# Paper 3: Taxation (Direct Tax & Goods and Services Tax)
# ICAI CA Intermediate Question Bank (New Scheme)

Chapter 1: Computation of Total Income & Tax Liability under Sec 115BAC vs Old Regime

Question 1. Dr. Rajesh Sharma (aged 48 years), a resident individual practicing as a consulting physician in Delhi, provides the following details of his financial transactions for the Previous Year 2024-25 (Assessment Year 2025-26):
1. Gross receipts from medical consulting profession: Rs. 42,50,000 (all received via crossed cheques or electronic clearing system).
2. Operating expenses claimed in Income & Expenditure account:
   - Clinic rent paid to landlord: Rs. 3,60,000.
   - Salary paid to clinic assistant: Rs. 2,40,000.
   - Purchase of diagnostic medical equipment on 10-11-2024 (put to use immediately): Rs. 4,00,000. (Normal depreciation rate applicable: 15%).
   - Cost of medicines purchased and administered: Rs. 6,20,000 (includes an invoice of Rs. 35,000 paid in physical cash on 14-08-2024).
3. Rental income from a commercial shop owned in Jaipur: Gross Rent received Rs. 35,000 per month. Municipal taxes paid by tenant: Rs. 15,000; Municipal taxes paid by Dr. Rajesh: Rs. 10,000. Interest paid on loan taken for construction of this shop: Rs. 85,000.
4. Long-term capital gains on sale of listed equity shares on 20-01-2025 (STT paid on acquisition and transfer): Rs. 2,25,000.
5. Savings bank account interest: Rs. 28,000.
6. Investments and payments made during the year:
   - Life insurance premium on own life (sum assured Rs. 5,00,000, policy issued in 2021): Rs. 45,000.
   - Contribution to Public Provident Fund (PPF): Rs. 1,00,000.
   - Health insurance premium paid via Net Banking for self and spouse: Rs. 28,000; for dependent father (aged 76 years, resident): Rs. 54,000.
   - Contribution to National Pension System (NPS Tier-I) under Section 80CCD(1B): Rs. 50,000.
Compute the Total Income and Net Tax Payable by Dr. Rajesh Sharma for AY 2025-26 under the Default Tax Regime under Section 115BAC(1A), and evaluate whether he should exercise the option to be taxed under the regular provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 14 | Time: 25 mins | Section 115BAC | PGBP & Total Income | High Yield | ICAI Fav]

Answer 1.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Finance Act Amendment note:
- Under Section 115BAC(1A), as substituted by Finance Act, the default tax regime applies to all Individuals, HUF, AOP, and BOI unless the assessee exercises an option to shift out under Section 115BAC(6).
- Under the Default Regime: Standard Chapter VI-A deductions (Sec 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B)) are disallowed. However, Section 80CCD(2) and Section 80JJAA remain available.
- Depreciation under Section 32: Medical equipment acquired and put to use for less than 180 days in the Previous Year (acquired 10-11-2024) is eligible for only 50% of the prescribed depreciation (i.e., 7.5% instead of 15%).
- Disallowance under Section 40A(3): Payment in cash exceeding Rs. 10,000 in a day for medicine expenditure is 100% disallowed.
- Capital Gains under Section 112A: Long-term capital gains on listed equity shares are taxable @ 10% on gains exceeding Rs. 1,00,000 (subject to recent Finance Act amendments).
- Section 87A Rebate: Under the Default Regime, resident individuals with total income not exceeding Rs. 7,00,000 qualify for rebate up to Rs. 25,000. Under the Old Regime, the rebate is restricted to Rs. 12,500 if total income does not exceed Rs. 5,00,000.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Total Income of Dr. Rajesh Sharma for Assessment Year 2025-26:

Working Note 1: Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP)
- Gross Professional Receipts: Rs. 42,50,000
Less: Allowable Professional Expenses:
- Clinic Rent: (Rs. 3,60,000)
- Salary to Clinic Assistant: (Rs. 2,40,000)
- Medicine Cost Allowable [Rs. 6,20,000 - Rs. 35,000 disallowed u/s 40A(3)]: (Rs. 5,85,000)
- Depreciation on Diagnostic Equipment [Rs. 4,00,000 * 15% * 50% (put to use < 180 days)]: (Rs. 30,000)
Net PGBP Income: Rs. 30,35,000

Working Note 2: Income from House Property (Commercial Shop - Jaipur)
- Gross Annual Value (Rent received Rs. 35,000 * 12): Rs. 4,20,000
- Less: Municipal taxes paid by Owner: (Rs. 10,000)
  (Note: Municipal taxes paid by tenant are not deductible)
- Net Annual Value (NAV): Rs. 4,10,000
- Less: Deductions under Section 24:
  (a) Standard deduction @ 30% of NAV: (Rs. 1,23,000)
  (b) Interest on borrowed capital u/s 24(b): (Rs. 85,000)
Net Income from House Property: Rs. 2,02,000

Working Note 3: Capital Gains
- Long-Term Capital Gains on listed equity shares (Sec 112A): Rs. 2,25,000

Working Note 4: Income from Other Sources
- Savings Bank Interest: Rs. 28,000

Gross Total Income (GTI):
= PGBP (Rs. 30,35,000) + House Property (Rs. 2,02,000) + Capital Gains (Rs. 2,25,000) + Other Sources (Rs. 28,000)
= Rs. 34,90,000

Step-Wise Comparison of Total Income & Tax Liability:
Particulars | Default Regime u/s 115BAC | Old / Optional Regime
--- | --- | ---
Gross Total Income | Rs. 34,90,000 | Rs. 34,90,000
Less: Deductions under Chapter VI-A: | |
- Section 80C (PPF Rs. 1,00,000 + LIC Rs. 45,000) | NIL | (Rs. 1,45,000)
- Section 80D (Self/Spouse: Max Rs. 25,000; Father: Max Rs. 50,000) | NIL | (Rs. 75,000)
- Section 80CCD(1B) (Additional NPS) | NIL | (Rs. 50,000)
- Section 80TTA (Savings interest capped at Rs. 10,000) | NIL | (Rs. 10,000)
Total Income (Rounded off u/s 288A) | Rs. 34,90,000 | Rs. 32,10,000

Tax Computation for AY 2025-26:
A. Under Default Regime (Sec 115BAC):
1. Tax on LTCG u/s 112A: 10% on (Rs. 2,25,000 - Rs. 1,00,000) = Rs. 12,500
2. Tax on Normal Income (Rs. 34,90,000 - Rs. 2,25,000 = Rs. 32,65,000):
   - Upto Rs. 3,00,000: NIL
   - Rs. 3,00,001 to Rs. 6,00,000 @ 5%: Rs. 15,000
   - Rs. 6,00,001 to Rs. 9,00,000 @ 10%: Rs. 30,000
   - Rs. 9,00,001 to Rs. 12,00,000 @ 15%: Rs. 45,000
   - Rs. 12,00,001 to Rs. 15,00,000 @ 20%: Rs. 60,000
   - Above Rs. 15,00,000: (Rs. 32,65,000 - Rs. 15,00,000) = Rs. 17,65,000 @ 30%: Rs. 5,29,500
   Subtotal Normal Tax: Rs. 6,79,500
Total Base Tax: Rs. 6,79,500 + Rs. 12,500 = Rs. 6,92,000
Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4%: Rs. 27,680
Total Tax Payable under Default Regime: Rs. 7,19,680

B. Under Old / Optional Normal Provisions:
1. Tax on LTCG u/s 112A: 10% on (Rs. 2,25,000 - Rs. 1,00,000) = Rs. 12,500
2. Tax on Balance Income (Rs. 32,10,000 - Rs. 2,25,000 = Rs. 29,85,000):
   - Upto Rs. 2,50,000: NIL
   - Rs. 2,50,001 to Rs. 5,00,000 @ 5%: Rs. 12,500
   - Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 @ 20%: Rs. 1,00,000
   - Above Rs. 10,00,000: (Rs. 29,85,000 - Rs. 10,00,000) = Rs. 19,85,000 @ 30%: Rs. 5,95,500
   Subtotal Normal Tax: Rs. 7,08,000
Total Base Tax: Rs. 7,08,000 + Rs. 12,500 = Rs. 7,20,500
Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4%: Rs. 28,820
Total Tax Payable under Old Regime: Rs. 7,49,320

3. Final Conclusion:
- Tax Payable under Default Regime (Section 115BAC): Rs. 7,19,680.
- Tax Payable under Old Regime: Rs. 7,49,320.
- The Default Tax Regime results in a net tax saving of Rs. 29,640 (Rs. 7,49,320 - Rs. 7,19,680). Therefore, Dr. Rajesh Sharma should remain in the Default Tax Regime under Section 115BAC(1A).

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always segregate special rate income (e.g., Section 112A LTCG @ 10%) from normal income before applying tax slab rates.
2. Remember that municipal taxes paid by the tenant are never deductible while computing NAV under Section 23.
3. Keep track of put-to-use dates: an asset put to use on or after October 4th (in a leap year) or October 3rd receives only 50% depreciation.

Examiner Observations:
1. Students frequently claim Chapter VI-A deductions under Section 115BAC by mistake; in the default regime, Section 80C, 80D, and 80TTA are completely barred.
2. Common error: failing to add back the cash payment of Rs. 35,000 under Section 40A(3).


Chapter 2: Profits & Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP)

Question 2. Apex Precision Tools Ltd., a resident manufacturing entity, reports a Net Profit of Rs. 78,50,000 in its Profit and Loss Account for the Previous Year ended 31st March 2025. A scrutiny of the accounts reveals the following details:
1. Depreciation debited to Profit and Loss Account is Rs. 11,20,000. Allowable depreciation as per Income-tax Rules is Rs. 14,80,000 (excluding additional depreciation on new machinery).
2. The company purchased and installed a new CNC Milling Machine costing Rs. 24,00,000 on 18-09-2024. Normal depreciation @ 15% is already included in the allowable figure, but additional depreciation under Section 32(1)(iia) was omitted.
3. Bonus to employees amounting to Rs. 4,50,000 was provided in the accounts on 31-03-2025. Out of this, Rs. 3,00,000 was paid on 10-09-2025, and Rs. 1,50,000 was paid on 15-11-2025. The statutory due date for filing return of income u/s 139(1) is 31st October 2025.
4. Professional fees paid to a technical consultant: Rs. 2,00,000 on which tax was not deducted at source during FY 2024-25. The TDS was deposited on 20-12-2025.
5. Outstanding trade payable to a Micro Enterprise registered under MSMED Act, 2006 for supply of raw materials: Rs. 3,80,000. The invoice was dated 15-01-2025 with no written agreed credit period. Payment was actually cleared on 25-04-2025.
6. Cash payment made to a single raw material transporter holding valid PAN and furnishing declaration under Section 194C(6): Rs. 28,000 in currency notes on 12-12-2024.
Compute the taxable income from Business and Profession for Assessment Year 2025-26, showing statutory adjustments with proper legal justification.

[Marks: 14 | Time: 25 mins | Section 43B(h) MSME | PGBP | Depreciation Sec 32 | ICAI Fav]

Answer 2.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Finance Act Amendment note:
- Section 32(1)(iia) Additional Depreciation: A manufacturing enterprise acquiring new plant and machinery is entitled to additional depreciation @ 20% of actual cost. Since the machinery was installed on 18-09-2024 (put to use for >= 180 days), 100% of the 20% rate (Rs. 4,80,000) is allowable.
- Section 43B Statutory Payments: Employee bonus is allowable on payment basis. Amounts paid on or before the due date of filing return under Section 139(1) (31-10-2025) are deductible in FY 2024-25. Amounts paid thereafter are allowed only in the year of actual payment.
- Section 40(a)(ia) 30% Disallowance: 30% of any sum payable to a resident on which TDS was deductible but not deducted, or deducted but not paid on or before the due date u/s 139(1), shall be disallowed.
- Section 43B(h) [Finance Act 2023 Amendment]: Any sum payable to a Micro or Small Enterprise beyond the time limit specified in Section 15 of MSMED Act (15 days in absence of written agreement, or maximum 45 days if agreed) is deductible ONLY in the previous year in which such sum is actually paid.
- Section 40A(3) read with Rule 6DD: Cash payments exceeding Rs. 10,000 are disallowed, except where payment is made to a goods transport operator, for whom the enhanced threshold is Rs. 35,000.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Business Income of Apex Precision Tools Ltd. for AY 2025-26:

Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Amount (Rs.)
--- | --- | ---
Net Profit as per Profit & Loss Account | | 78,50,000
Add: Inadmissible Expenses / Separate Disallowances: | |
1. Depreciation debited to P&L Account (to be recalculated u/s 32) | 11,20,000 |
2. Employee Bonus paid after due date of filing return u/s 139(1) [Paid 15-11-2025; disallowed u/s 43B] | 1,50,000 |
3. Disallowance u/s 40(a)(ia) for non-deduction of TDS on professional fees [30% of Rs. 2,00,000] | 60,000 |
4. Overdue payment to Micro Enterprise u/s 43B(h) [Invoice date 15-01-2025; 15-day limit expired on 30-01-2025; payment made on 25-04-2025 falls in subsequent FY 2025-26] | 3,80,000 |
5. Cash payment to transporter: Rs. 28,000 is within the enhanced ceiling of Rs. 35,000 u/s 40A(3) -> No disallowance | NIL |
Subtotal Additions | | 17,10,000
Gross Adjusted Profit | | 95,60,000
Less: Allowable Deductions: | |
1. Depreciation allowable as per Income-tax Rules (excluding additional) | (14,80,000) |
2. Additional Depreciation u/s 32(1)(iia) on new CNC Milling Machine [Rs. 24,00,000 * 20%] | (4,80,000) |
Subtotal Deductions | | (19,60,000)
Taxable Profits and Gains of Business or Profession | | 76,00,000

Working Notes:
- Working Note 1 on Section 43B(h): Section 15 of MSMED Act mandates payment within 15 days when there is no written contract. The statutory liability was due on 30-01-2025. Because it was not settled within FY 2024-25, Section 43B(h) prohibits deduction on accrual basis. It will be allowed as deduction in FY 2025-26 when paid.
- Working Note 2 on Section 40(a)(ia): The TDS was deposited on 20-12-2025, which is after the return filing due date (31-10-2025). Therefore, 30% of Rs. 2,00,000 (Rs. 60,000) is added back.
- Working Note 3 on Employee Bonus: Out of Rs. 4,50,000 bonus, Rs. 3,00,000 paid on 10-09-2025 is allowed since it was settled prior to 31-10-2025.

3. Final Conclusion:
- The Taxable Income from Business and Profession for Assessment Year 2025-26 is Rs. 76,00,000.
- Apex Precision Tools Ltd. can claim the disallowed MSME payment of Rs. 3,80,000 and the 30% TDS disallowance of Rs. 60,00,00 in the subsequent Assessment Year 2026-27 upon fulfillment of payment and deposit obligations.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Create clear columns for "Disallowed Additions" and "Permissible Deductions" to prevent mathematical sign errors.
2. Remember that for goods transport operators, Section 40A(3) threshold is Rs. 35,000 instead of Rs. 10,000.
3. Section 43B(h) cannot be rescued by paying before the Section 139(1) due date; it MUST be paid within the MSMED Act time limit (15 or 45 days).

Examiner Observations:
1. Examinees routinely fail to apply the newly introduced Section 43B(h) rule and treat MSME payments identically to ordinary Section 43B items.
2. In additional depreciation, verify the date of installation; put to use before October 4 grants full 20%, whereas post-October 3 gives 10% in the current year and 10% in the next.


Chapter 3: Capital Gains (Sec 45 to Sec 55)

Question 3. Mr. Anand (aged 52 years, resident) purchased a residential house property in Pune on 15-06-2005 for Rs. 18,00,000. On 12-08-2024, he sold this residential house for a declared consideration of Rs. 92,00,000. The stamp duty valuation of the property on the date of agreement (10-06-2024) was Rs. 98,00,000, and on the date of registration (12-08-2024), it was Rs. 1,02,00,000. An advance of Rs. 5,00,000 was received by Mr. Anand via account payee bank draft on 10-06-2024.
Mr. Anand incurred brokerage expenses of 1% on the sale consideration.
To save tax on capital gains, Mr. Anand carried out the following transactions:
1. Purchased a new residential flat in Mumbai on 14-01-2025 for Rs. 45,00,000.
2. Invested Rs. 30,00,000 on 10-02-2025 in capital gain bonds issued by the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) under Section 54EC.
Cost Inflation Index (CII): FY 2005-06 = 117; FY 2024-25 = 363.
Compute the Capital Gains chargeable to tax for Assessment Year 2025-26 under the amended provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 10 | Time: 18 mins | Section 50C Safe Harbour | Section 54 Exemption | Section 54EC Bonds]

Answer 3.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 50C Full Value of Consideration: Where the consideration declared is less than the Stamp Duty Value (SDV), SDV is adopted as full value of consideration. However, under the safe harbour rule, if SDV does not exceed 110% of the actual consideration, the actual consideration is taken.
- Date of Agreement vs Date of Registration: Where part or whole of the consideration was received by account payee cheque/draft or ECS on or before the date of agreement, the SDV on the date of agreement (Rs. 98,00,000) shall be compared with consideration.
- Holding Period: Property acquired on 15-06-2005 and sold on 12-08-2024 exceeds 24 months, hence it is a Long-Term Capital Asset.
- Exemption under Section 54: Available on transfer of a long-term residential house if capital gains are reinvested in one residential house in India within 1 year before or 2 years after transfer.
- Exemption under Section 54EC: Reinvestment in specified bonds (NHAI/REC/PFC) within 6 months from the date of transfer, subject to a maximum cap of Rs. 50,00,000.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Long-Term Capital Gains of Mr. Anand for Assessment Year 2025-26:

Step 1: Determination of Full Value of Consideration under Section 50C
- Actual declared consideration: Rs. 92,00,000
- 110% of declared consideration = Rs. 92,00,000 * 1.10 = Rs. 1,01,20,000
- Advance was received by Account Payee Bank Draft on the agreement date (10-06-2024). Hence, SDV as on the agreement date (Rs. 98,00,000) is taken for evaluation.
- Since SDV of Rs. 98,00,000 <= Rs. 1,01,20,000 (110% of actual consideration), the safe harbour applies.
- Therefore, Full Value of Consideration = Rs. 92,00,000.

Step 2: Transfer Expenses
- Brokerage paid @ 1% on Rs. 92,00,000 = Rs. 92,000.
- Net Sale Consideration = Rs. 92,00,000 - Rs. 92,00,000 = Rs. 91,08,000.

Step 3: Cost of Acquisition
- Cost of acquisition = Rs. 18,00,000.
(Under the amended tax framework for land/building acquired prior to 23-07-2024, an individual resident assessee may compute tax under normal indexed cost @ 20% or unindexed cost @ 12.5%, whichever is lower).
- Indexed Cost of Acquisition = Rs. 18,00,000 * (363 / 117) = Rs. 55,84,615.

Step 4: Gross Long-Term Capital Gains
- Gross LTCG = Net Consideration (Rs. 91,08,000) - Indexed Cost (Rs. 55,84,615) = Rs. 35,23,385.
(Alternatively, under unindexed cost: Rs. 91,08,000 - Rs. 18,00,000 = Rs. 73,08,000).

Step 5: Deductions under Section 54 and Section 54EC
- Exemption under Section 54: Cost of new residential flat purchased within 2 years (14-01-2025) = Rs. 45,00,000.
- Exemption is limited to the available Gross Long-Term Capital Gains.
- Since Gross LTCG is Rs. 35,23,385, Section 54 exemption absorbs the entire gain: Rs. 35,23,385.
- Exemption under Section 54EC: Rs. 30,00,000 invested in NHAI bonds within 6 months is valid, but the taxable capital gain is already reduced to NIL by Section 54.

3. Final Conclusion:
- The Taxable Long-Term Capital Gain chargeable to tax for Assessment Year 2025-26 is NIL.
- The safe harbour rule of 110% under Section 50C protected the transaction from stamp duty enhancement, and Section 54 reinvestment fully shielded the capital gains.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always test the 110% safe harbour rule under Section 50C before substituting SDV for sale consideration.
2. Check whether advance was received through banking channels on or before the agreement date to freeze SDV to agreement date.
3. Remember that total exemption under Section 54 cannot exceed the actual capital gains generated.

Examiner Observations:
1. Students frequently use SDV on registration date even when an account payee instrument was cleared on the agreement date.
2. Forgetting that brokerage is calculated on actual consideration, not stamp duty valuation.


Chapter 4: Income from Salaries & Perquisites

Question 4. Mrs. Suniti Roy is an Executive Vice President at Zeta Pharmaceuticals Ltd., Kolkata (population > 40 lakhs). Her emoluments for the Previous Year 2024-25 are as follows:
1. Basic Salary: Rs. 1,80,000 per month.
2. Dearness Allowance (forming part of retirement benefits): 40% of basic salary.
3. Bonus received during the year: Rs. 3,50,000.
4. Rent-Free Accommodation: The company provided her with an unfurnished accommodation owned by the employer in Kolkata for the entire year.
5. Motor Car: The company provided an engine capacity 1.8-litre car owned by the employer, along with a chauffeur. All running and maintenance expenses (including petrol and driver's salary) were borne by the company. The car is utilized for both official and private purposes.
6. Children Education Allowance: Rs. 800 per month received for her two children.
7. Professional tax paid: Rs. 2,500 (out of which Rs. 1,500 was paid by the employer on her behalf).
Compute her taxable income under the head "Salaries" for Assessment Year 2025-26 under the Default Regime under Section 115BAC and under the Old Regime.

[Marks: 10 | Time: 18 mins | Salaries Sec 15-17 | Rule 3 Car & Accommodation | Standard Deduction]

Answer 4.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Valuation of Rent-Free Accommodation (Rule 3): Under the amended Rule 3, where accommodation is owned by the employer in a city having population exceeding 40 lakhs as per 2011 census (Kolkata), perquisite value is 10% of "Salary" (reduced from previous 15%).
- "Salary" for accommodation perquisite includes: Basic + DA (forming part) + Bonus + all taxable allowances.
- Motor Car Perquisite (Rule 3): For a car exceeding 1.6 litres capacity used for both official and personal use with employer bearing all expenses: Perquisite value is Rs. 2,400 per month + Rs. 900 per month for chauffeur = Rs. 3,300 per month.
- Children Education Allowance: Under Section 10(14), exemption is up to Rs. 100 per month per child for maximum 2 children (Only under Old Regime; completely taxable under default Section 115BAC).
- Standard Deduction under Section 16(ia): Rs. 50,000 (or Rs. 75,000 under amended 115BAC). Professional tax paid under Section 16(iii) is deductible only in the Old Regime.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Salary Income for Assessment Year 2025-26:

Salary Components:
- Basic Salary: Rs. 1,80,000 * 12 = Rs. 21,60,000
- Dearness Allowance (forming part): 40% of Rs. 21,60,000 = Rs. 8,64,000
- Bonus: Rs. 3,50,000

Children Education Allowance:
- Total received: Rs. 800 * 12 = Rs. 9,600
- Under Default Regime: Fully taxable = Rs. 9,600
- Under Old Regime: Exempt u/s 10(14) [Rs. 100 * 2 children * 12 = Rs. 2,400]. Taxable = Rs. 7,200.

Perquisite 1: Rent-Free Accommodation (Rule 3)
"Salary" for Accommodation:
= Basic (Rs. 21,60,000) + DA (Rs. 8,64,000) + Bonus (Rs. 3,50,000) + Taxable Allowance (Rs. 9,600 / Rs. 7,200)
= Rs. 33,83,600 (under 115BAC) / Rs. 33,81,200 (under Old Regime)
- Perquisite value @ 10% (Population > 40 lakhs):
  - Under 115BAC: 10% of Rs. 33,83,600 = Rs. 3,38,360
  - Under Old Regime: 10% of Rs. 33,81,200 = Rs. 3,38,120

Perquisite 2: Motor Car (> 1.6 litres + Driver + Company borne expenses)
- Car perquisite: Rs. 2,400 * 12 = Rs. 28,800
- Chauffeur perquisite: Rs. 900 * 12 = Rs. 10,800
- Total car perquisite = Rs. 39,600 (same in both regimes)

Perquisite 3: Professional tax paid by employer
- Added to Gross Salary = Rs. 1,500

Comparative Summary of Salary:
Particulars | Default Regime u/s 115BAC (Rs.) | Old Regime (Rs.)
--- | --- | ---
Basic Salary | 21,60,000 | 21,60,000
Dearness Allowance | 8,64,000 | 8,64,000
Bonus | 3,50,000 | 3,50,000
Children Education Allowance | 9,600 | 7,200
Perquisite for Accommodation | 3,38,360 | 3,38,120
Perquisite for Motor Car | 39,600 | 39,600
Employer contribution to Professional Tax | 1,500 | 1,500
Gross Salary | 37,63,060 | 37,60,420
Less: Deductions under Section 16: | |
- Standard Deduction u/s 16(ia) | (75,000) | (50,000)
- Professional Tax u/s 16(iii) | NIL | (2,500)
Net Taxable Salary | 36,88,060 | 37,07,920

3. Final Conclusion:
- Taxable Income under the head "Salaries" is Rs. 36,88,060 under the Default Regime, and Rs. 37,07,920 under the Old Regime.
- The enhanced standard deduction under Section 115BAC coupled with the 10% accommodation rate gives a substantial tax benefit under the default regime.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Note the amended Rule 3 accommodation percentages: 10% for cities > 40 lakhs, 7.5% for 15-40 lakhs, and 5% for other cities.
2. Employer-paid professional tax must first be added to gross salary as a perquisite, and then deducted under Section 16(iii) (under Old Regime).
3. Motor car running expenses borne by employer for personal + official use trigger fixed statutory values without requiring logbook analysis.

Examiner Observations:
1. Students still apply the superseded 15% rate for employer-owned accommodation instead of the amended 10% rate.
2. Forgetting that Section 16(iii) professional tax deduction is unavailable under Section 115BAC.


Chapter 5: Income from House Property & Inter-Head Loss Adjustment

Question 5. Mr. Vikram owns two houses in Bengaluru. He furnishes the following information for the Previous Year 2024-25:
- House 1: Occupied by Mr. Vikram for his own residence throughout the year. Municipal valuation is Rs. 4,50,000; Fair Rent is Rs. 4,80,000; Standard Rent is Rs. 4,20,000. Municipal taxes paid: Rs. 24,000. He borrowed Rs. 25,00,000 on 01-07-2020 @ 10% p.a. for construction of this house (construction completed on 15-02-2022). Interest paid during the current year is Rs. 2,35,000.
- House 2: Let out at a monthly rent of Rs. 30,000. Municipal valuation is Rs. 3,20,000; Fair Rent is Rs. 3,60,000; Standard Rent is Rs. 3,50,000. Municipal taxes of Rs. 20,000 were assessed, but only Rs. 12,000 was actually paid by Mr. Vikram during the year. Tenant vacated on 31-12-2024 without paying rent for 2 months (November and December 2024). All conditions of Rule 4 are satisfied. The house remained vacant for the remaining 3 months of the year despite reasonable efforts to let it out.
Interest paid on housing loan for House 2 is Rs. 1,40,000.
Mr. Vikram also has income from salary of Rs. 12,00,000.
Compute the Income from House Property and Gross Total Income of Mr. Vikram for Assessment Year 2025-26 under the regular provisions, keeping in mind the inter-head set-off restrictions under Section 71(3A).

[Marks: 8 | Time: 14 mins | House Property Sec 22-27 | Sec 24(b) | Inter-Head Set-Off Sec 71(3A)]

Answer 5.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Self-Occupied Property (SOP): Under Section 23(2), the Annual Value of one or two self-occupied properties is taken as NIL.
- Interest on housing loan for SOP (Section 24(b)): Deduction for self-occupied property is limited to a maximum of Rs. 2,00,000 if loan was taken on or after 01-04-1999 for acquisition/construction completed within 5 years.
- Let-Out Property (LOP): Annual Value is computed under Section 23(1). Municipal taxes actually paid by owner during the year are deducted. Standard deduction @ 30% is allowed under Section 24(a). Entire interest is deductible without upper cap.
- Unrealized Rent (Rule 4): Deducted from Actual Rent Receivable if tenant vacated and legal remedies are pursued.

Chapter 6: Clubbing of Income & Aggregation (Sec 60 to 65)

Question 6. During the Previous Year 2024-25, Mr. Kameshwar (a resident individual having total business income of Rs. 16,00,000) details the following transactions:
1. On 10-05-2024, he gifted Rs. 6,00,000 in cash to his wife, Mrs. Priya. Mrs. Priya invested this entire money in 10% Fixed Deposits with a nationalized bank. Interest earned during the year was Rs. 50,000. On 01-01-2025, she reinvested this interest of Rs. 50,000 in shares, earning a dividend of Rs. 4,000 before 31st March 2025.
2. He gifted 1,000 debentures of an Indian company (cost Rs. 2,00,000) to his son's wife (daughter-in-law) on 15-06-2024 without consideration. Interest received on debentures was Rs. 24,000.
3. His minor daughter, Ananya (aged 14 years), won a prize of Rs. 1,20,000 in an all-India classical dance competition. The prize money was deposited in a bank savings account which earned interest of Rs. 8,000.
4. His minor son, Kabir (aged 11 years), received a gift of Rs. 80,000 from his maternal grandfather, which was invested in a fixed deposit, earning interest of Rs. 6,500 during the year.
5. Mrs. Priya's total income (excluding clubbed items) is Rs. 3,50,000.
Determine the total taxable income of Mr. Kameshwar and Mrs. Priya for Assessment Year 2025-26 under the provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 14 mins | Clubbing Sec 64 | Minor Income Sec 64(1A) | Talent Exception]

Answer 6.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 64(1)(iv) Transfer to Spouse: Income arising directly or indirectly from assets transferred to spouse without adequate consideration is clubbed in the hands of the transferor spouse. However, income earned from the accretion of such income (interest on interest / reinvested income) is NOT clubbed.
- Section 64(1)(vi) Transfer to Daughter-in-Law: Income from assets transferred to son's wife without adequate consideration is directly clubbed in the hands of the transferor.
- Section 64(1A) Minor Child Income: All income accruing to a minor child is clubbed in the hands of that parent whose total income (excluding minor's income) is higher.
  - Exception 1: Income earned by a minor on account of manual work or any activity involving application of talent, specialized knowledge, or skill is NOT clubbed.
  - Exception 2: Interest earned on deposit of such prize money does not involve personal skill directly, hence it is clubbed.
  - Exemption under Section 10(32): Exemption of up to Rs. 1,500 per minor child is allowed to the parent in whose income the minor's income is clubbed (available under Old Regime; note that under Section 115BAC, Section 10(32) exemption is not available).

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Taxable Income for Assessment Year 2025-26:

Working Notes on Clubbing Allocations:
1. Gift to Wife (Mrs. Priya):
   - Bank FD interest on gifted principal: Rs. 50,000 is directly clubbed in the hands of Mr. Kameshwar u/s 64(1)(iv).
   - Dividend from reinvestment of interest: Rs. 4,000 is income on accretion. It is NOT clubbed and remains taxable in the hands of Mrs. Priya.
2. Gift to Daughter-in-Law:
   - Interest of Rs. 24,000 on debentures transferred without consideration is clubbed in the hands of Mr. Kameshwar u/s 64(1)(vi).
3. Minor Daughter (Ananya):
   - Dance prize money (Rs. 1,20,000) was won through specialized talent and skill. It is NOT clubbed and is assessed directly in the hands of minor daughter through legal guardian.
   - Bank interest of Rs. 8,000 on deposited prize money is clubbed in the hands of Mr. Kameshwar (since his income Rs. 16,00,000 > Mrs. Priya's income Rs. 3,50,000).
   - Exemption u/s 10(32) of Rs. 1,500 is deducted: Net clubbed = Rs. 6,500.
4. Minor Son (Kabir):
   - Fixed deposit interest of Rs. 6,500 is clubbed in the hands of Mr. Kameshwar.
   - Exemption u/s 10(32) of Rs. 1,500 is deducted: Net clubbed = Rs. 5,000.

Summary of Taxable Income:
A. Computation of Total Income of Mr. Kameshwar:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.)
--- | ---
Own Business Income | 16,00,000
Clubbed Income: |
- Interest on bank FD gifted to wife u/s 64(1)(iv) | 50,000
- Interest on debentures gifted to daughter-in-law u/s 64(1)(vi) | 24,00,000 -> 24,000
- Minor daughter Ananya's bank interest [Rs. 8,000 - Rs. 1,500 u/s 10(32)] | 6,500
- Minor son Kabir's FD interest [Rs. 6,500 - Rs. 1,500 u/s 10(32)] | 5,000
Total Taxable Income of Mr. Kameshwar | 16,85,500

B. Computation of Total Income of Mrs. Priya:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.)
--- | ---
Own Income | 3,50,000
Dividend income from reinvestment of accumulated interest | 4,000
Total Taxable Income of Mrs. Priya | 3,54,000

3. Final Conclusion:
- Total Income of Mr. Kameshwar: Rs. 16,85,500.
- Total Income of Mrs. Priya: Rs. 3,54,000.
- Income on accretion of gifted funds is strictly taxable in the hands of the transferee, and talent-based income of minors is completely outside the ambit of parental clubbing.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always compare parent incomes before clubbing minor's income; once clubbed with one parent, it continues to be clubbed with that parent in subsequent years unless AO directs otherwise.
2. Double-check Section 10(32): Rs. 1,500 per child is available for each minor child separately.
3. Accretion to transferred asset (e.g. interest earned on interest or bonus shares on gifted shares) does not attract clubbing.

Examiner Observations:
1. Clubbing the minor's talent prize money is a frequent student blunder.
2. Forgetting that transfers to son's wife are specifically covered by Section 64(1)(vi).

Condition Check:
- Net Agricultural Income = Rs. 4,50,000 (> Rs. 5,000).
- Non-Agricultural Income = Rs. 9,06,000 (> Basic Exemption Limit of Rs. 2,50,000).
Hence, Partial Integration applies.

Step A: Add Non-Agricultural Income and Net Agricultural Income
= Rs. 9,06,000 + Rs. 4,50,000 = Rs. 13,56,000.

- Upto Rs. 2,50,000: NIL

- Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 @ 20%: Rs. 1,00,000
- Above Rs. 10,00,000: (Rs. 13,56,000 - Rs. 10,00,000) = Rs. 3,56,000 @ 30%: Rs. 1,06,800

Chapter 9: Tax Deduction at Source (TDS) and TCS

Question 9. Examine the liability for Tax Deduction at Source (TDS) / Tax Collection at Source (TCS) in the following independent transactions during the Financial Year 2024-25, quoting relevant sections, applicable thresholds, and rates:
1. Orbit Infrastructures Ltd. pays Rs. 48,00,000 to Mr. Somnath, an individual transport contractor who owns 7 goods carriages throughout the year and produces his PAN along with declaration under Section 194C(6).
2. Modern Polyfilms Pvt. Ltd. (turnover in preceding FY: Rs. 14 Crores) purchases plastic granules from Reliance Petrochemicals Ltd. for Rs. 72,00,000 on 12-10-2024. Payment of Rs. 72,00,000 was made on 20-10-2024. Both buyer and seller are liable for TDS/TCS under Sections 194Q and 206C(1H).
3. Beta Technologies Ltd. pays Rs. 2,80,000 to Mr. Alok for routine IT software maintenance services, and Rs. 4,50,000 to Dr. Sen (Architect) for structural building designs.
4. Zenith Retailers Ltd. pays cash of Rs. 1,15,00,000 on various dates during the FY to a wholesale supplier from its current account. The recipient has regularly filed returns of income for all past 3 assessment years.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 14 mins | TDS Sec 194C/J/Q/N | TCS Sec 206C(1H) | High Yield]

Answer 9.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 194C(6) Transport Contractor Exemption: No deduction of tax shall be made from any sum credited or paid to a contractor during the course of business of plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages, if the contractor owns 10 or fewer goods carriages at any time during the previous year and furnishes a declaration to that effect along with PAN.
- Section 194Q vs Section 206C(1H): Section 194Q mandates that any buyer whose turnover in the preceding financial year exceeded Rs. 10 Crores shall deduct TDS @ 0.1% on purchase of goods from a resident seller exceeding Rs. 50 Lakhs in a financial year. If Section 194Q applies, Section 206C(1H) (TCS) does not apply.
- Section 194J Professional vs Technical Fees: Under Section 194J, TDS on technical services / royalty is @ 2%, while TDS on professional fees is @ 10% (threshold: Rs. 30,000 per year per category).
- Section 194N Cash Withdrawals: TDS @ 2% is deductible on aggregate cash withdrawals from one or more accounts maintained with a banking company exceeding Rs. 1 Crore during the previous year for an assessee who is a regular return filer.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Analysis of Transactions:

1. Orbit Infrastructures Ltd. to Mr. Somnath:
- Mr. Somnath owns 7 goods carriages (which is <= 10 carriages) and furnished a valid declaration along with his PAN.
- As per Section 194C(6), no TDS is deductible.
- Orbit Infrastructures Ltd. must report the transaction in its TDS return (Form 26Q).

2. Modern Polyfilms Pvt. Ltd. (Buyer) and Reliance Petrochemicals Ltd. (Seller):
- Turnover of buyer in preceding FY exceeded Rs. 10 Crores (Rs. 14 Crores).
- Value of goods purchased exceeds Rs. 50 Lakhs: Excess = Rs. 72,00,000 - Rs. 50,00,000 = Rs. 22,00,000.
- Section 194Q overrides Section 206C(1H). Therefore, the buyer (Modern Polyfilms Pvt. Ltd.) must deduct TDS @ 0.1% on the excess Rs. 22,00,000 at the time of credit or payment, whichever is earlier.
- TDS Amount = Rs. 22,00,000 * 0.1% = Rs. 2,200.
- Seller is not required to collect TCS under Section 206C(1H).

3. Beta Technologies Ltd.:
- IT Software maintenance falls under the category of "Fees for Technical Services" (FTS). Tax is deductible @ 2% u/s 194J(1).
  - Threshold of Rs. 30,000 is exceeded (Rs. 2,80,000).
  - TDS = Rs. 2,80,000 * 2% = Rs. 5,600.
- Architectural fees paid to Dr. Sen constitute "Professional Fees". Tax is deductible @ 10% u/s 194J(1).
  - Threshold of Rs. 30,00,000 -> Rs. 30,000 is exceeded (Rs. 4,50,000).
  - TDS = Rs. 4,50,000 * 10% = Rs. 45,000.

4. Cash Withdrawal of Rs. 1,15,00,000:
- The recipient has regularly filed returns for all 3 preceding assessment years.
- Under Section 194N, TDS applies on cash withdrawals exceeding Rs. 1 Crore.
- Excess over Rs. 1 Crore = Rs. 1,15,00,000 - Rs. 1,00,00,000 = Rs. 15,00,000.
- Applicable TDS rate = 2%.
- TDS Amount to be deducted by Bank = Rs. 15,00,000 * 2% = Rs. 30,000.

3. Final Conclusion:
- Transaction 1: NIL TDS u/s 194C(6).
- Transaction 2: Buyer must deduct TDS of Rs. 2,200 u/s 194Q; no TCS u/s 206C(1H).
- Transaction 3: TDS of Rs. 5,600 @ 2% on FTS, and TDS of Rs. 45,000 @ 10% on professional fees u/s 194J.
- Transaction 4: Bank must deduct TDS of Rs. 30,000 @ 2% on Rs. 15,00,000 u/s 194N.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always state the priority rule between Section 194Q and 206C(1H): Section 194Q takes statutory precedence.
2. Differentiate between FTS (2%) and Professional fees (10%) under Section 194J.
3. Section 194N applies only to the excess over Rs. 1 Crore for regular filers, but the threshold drops to Rs. 20 Lakhs for non-filers.

Examiner Observations:
1. Applying 10% rate on all Section 194J payments, failing to recognize the reduced 2% rate for FTS and royalty.
2. Calculating TDS u/s 194Q on the full gross amount rather than the amount exceeding Rs. 50 Lakhs.

Establishments of the same person in different States having separate GST registrations are deemed "distinct persons".
- Section 7(2) read with Schedule III (Paragraph 1): Services by an employee to the employer in the course of or in relation to his employment are treated neither as a supply of goods nor as a supply of services. However, services rendered outside the employment contract for independent remuneration are taxable supplies.
- Schedule I (Paragraph 1): Permanent transfer or disposal of business assets where input tax credit has been availed on such assets is deemed a supply even without consideration.
- Section 7(1)(aa) [Retrospective Amendment]: The supply of activities or transactions, by a person (other than an individual) to its members or vice versa, for cash, deferred payment or other valuable consideration is an explicit supply.
- Schedule III (Paragraph 6): Actionable claims other than specified actionable claims (betting, casinos, gambling, lottery, online money gaming) are neither a supply of goods nor a supply of services.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Analysis of Activities:

1. Transfer of Smartphones from Mumbai HO to Ahmedabad Branch:
- Mumbai HO and Ahmedabad Branch are registered in different States under the same PAN, making them "Distinct Persons" under Section 25(4).
- The transfer is in the course or furtherance of business.
- As per Schedule I, Paragraph 2, this transaction constitutes a deemed "Supply of Goods" under Section 7(1)(c), even though made without consideration.
- GST is payable on the open market value or as per Rule 28 of CGST Rules.

2. Accounting Consultancy by Employee outside Contract:
- Under Schedule III (Paragraph 1), only services rendered in the course of employment are excluded from GST.
- Here, Pradeep provides consultancy outside employment hours for separate professional consideration.
- Therefore, it falls outside Schedule III and constitutes a taxable "Supply of Services" under Section 7(1)(a).

3. Free Permanent Transfer of Machinery by Zenith Steels Ltd.:
- Zenith Steels Ltd. availed ITC on the machinery when purchased.
- As per Schedule I, Paragraph 1, permanent transfer or disposal of business assets on which ITC has been availed constitutes a deemed "Supply of Goods" under Section 7(1)(c), even in the absence of consideration.

4. Facilities by High Flyers Club to its Members:
- Section 7(1)(aa) explicitly brings transactions between a club/association and its members within the definition of supply.
- The doctrine of mutuality has been statutorily eliminated in GST.
- Hence, the provision of catering and recreation for subscription fees is a taxable "Supply of Services".

5. Actionable Claims Classification:
- Unsecured trade debts are normal actionable claims covered by Schedule III (Paragraph 6) -> Neither a supply of goods nor a supply of services (Not liable to GST).
- Lottery tickets, betting, gambling, and online money gaming are specifically excluded from Schedule III exemption -> They constitute a taxable "Supply of Goods".

3. Final Conclusion:
- Activity 1: Taxable Supply of Goods under Schedule I.
- Activity 2: Taxable Supply of Services under Section 7(1)(a).
- Activity 3: Taxable Supply of Goods under Schedule I.
- Activity 4: Taxable Supply of Services under Section 7(1)(aa).
- Activity 5: Unsecured trade debt is Non-Supply (Schedule III); Lottery/Betting is Taxable Supply of Goods.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always test distinct person status (Section 25) whenever goods move between branches in different states.
2. Remember that Schedule I Paragraph 1 requires ITC to have been availed; if no ITC was claimed on the asset, gifting it without consideration is NOT a supply.
3. Check whether services performed by an employee are within or outside the contractual terms of employment.

Examiner Observations:
1. Misclassifying inter-branch stock transfers as non-supplies on the grounds that no sale occurred.
2. Overlooking Section 7(1)(aa) and applying the old common law principle of mutuality to clubs.

Chapter 12: Value of Taxable Supply (Section 15 & Determination of Value)

Question 12. M/s Shaurya Electricals Ltd., an air conditioner manufacturer in Pune, supplies 100 units of Commercial Air Conditioners to Cool Comforts Ltd., an unrelated dealer in Mumbai. The agreed list price per AC unit is Rs. 32,000 (exclusive of taxes). The following additional transactions relate to this supply contract:
1. Special durable protective packaging was provided at the buyer's request: Rs. 800 per unit.
2. Freight and transit insurance charged separately on the invoice: Rs. 65,000 for the entire consignment.
3. Municipal Corporation of Pune levied local Octroi / entry cess of Rs. 15,000 on the movement of goods, which was charged by Shaurya Electricals Ltd. to the buyer.
4. Cool Comforts Ltd. delayed payment by 45 days. As per contract terms, Shaurya Electricals Ltd. recovered delayed payment interest of Rs. 23,600 (inclusive of GST @ 18%).
5. Shaurya Electricals Ltd. received a direct price subsidy of Rs. 1,500 per AC unit from a Green Energy Foundation (a registered private trust promoting power-efficient appliances). This subsidy was directly linked to the price of the ACs and deducted from the invoice price.
6. A trade discount of 5% on the list price was allowed at the time of supply and recorded on the face of the tax invoice.
Determine the Value of Taxable Supply and the total GST payable (CGST @ 9% and SGST @ 9%, or IGST @ 18%) under Section 15 of the CGST Act, 2017.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 14 mins | Value of Supply Sec 15 | Discounts Sec 15(3) | Subsidies Sec 15(2)]

Answer 12.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 15(1) Transaction Value: The value of supply shall be the transaction value, which is the price actually paid or payable for the supply of goods where supplier and recipient are not related and price is the sole consideration.
- Inclusions under Section 15(2):
  - (a) Any taxes, duties, cesses, and fees levied under any law other than CGST, SGST, UTGST, and GST Compensation Cess.
  - (c) Incidental expenses, including commission and packing, charged by the supplier to the recipient.
  - (d) Interest, late fee, or penalty for delayed payment of consideration.
  - (e) Subsidies directly linked to the price, EXCLUDING subsidies provided by the Central Government and State Governments. Subsidies from private trusts must be added back.
- Exclusions under Section 15(3): Discounts given before or at the time of supply are deductible if duly recorded in the invoice.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Value of Taxable Supply under Section 15:

Particulars | Computation | Amount (Rs.)
--- | --- | ---
List Price for 100 AC units | 100 units * Rs. 32,00,000 / 100 = | 32,00,000
Less: Trade Discount allowed at invoice time u/s 15(3)(a) | 5% of Rs. 32,00,000 | (1,60,000)
Net Base Price | | 30,40,000
Add: Inclusions under Section 15(2): | |
1. Special packaging charges u/s 15(2)(c) | 100 units * Rs. 800 | 80,000
2. Freight and transit insurance u/s 15(2)(c) | Charged on invoice | 65,000
3. Municipal Octroi / Local cess u/s 15(2)(a) | Taxes other than GST | 15,000
4. Non-Government Subsidy from Private Trust u/s 15(2)(e) | 100 units * Rs. 1,500 | 1,50,000
5. Delayed payment interest u/s 15(2)(d) [Exclusive of GST: Rs. 23,600 * 100 / 118] | Interest is cum-tax as per Rule 35 | 20,000
Total Value of Taxable Supply | | 33,70,000

Nature of Supply and Tax Determination:
- Supplier location: Pune (Maharashtra)
- Place of Supply: Mumbai (Maharashtra)
- Transaction is an Intra-State Supply -> CGST and SGST apply.

Tax Computation:
- CGST @ 9% on Rs. 33,70,000 = Rs. 3,03,300
- SGST @ 9% on Rs. 33,70,000 = Rs. 3,03,300
Total GST Payable = Rs. 6,06,600.

Working Notes:
1. Delayed Payment Interest: Under Section 12(6), the time of supply for delayed interest is the date of receipt. Since interest was received with GST, it must be de-grossed using the formula: [Amount * 100 / (100 + GST Rate)] = Rs. 23,600 * 100 / 118 = Rs. 20,000.
2. Private Subsidy: Only Central and State Government subsidies are excluded from the transaction value. Subsidy from a private trust directly linked to the price must be included.

1. Remember to treat late payment interest as inclusive of GST unless explicitly stated otherwise.

3. Check the locations of supplier and place of supply to correctly charge CGST + SGST vs IGST.

1. Adding the entire Rs. 23,600 interest without de-grossing the GST component.
2. Deducting private trust subsidies from the transaction value.

Question 13. Determine the "Time of Supply" under the CGST Act, 2017 in each of the following independent situations, providing statutory explanations:

Karan Enterprises, a registered manufacturer of furniture, enters into a contract with a client for supply of office workstations.

- Date of advance payment received in bank: 05-11-2024 (Rs. 1,00,000).

Mayur Chemicals Ltd., a registered entity in Gujarat, purchases raw chemical materials from an unregistered agriculturist:
- Date of receipt of goods: 14-09-2024.
- Date of payment entered in books of account: 20-09-2024.

Answer 13.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 12(2) Time of Supply of Goods (Forward Charge) read with Notification No. 66/2017-CT: Registered persons supplying goods (other than composition dealers) are exempt from payment of GST on advances. Hence, time of supply of goods is the date of issue of invoice or the last date on which the invoice is required to be issued under Section 31(1), whichever is earlier.
- Section 31(1): Invoice for goods involving movement must be issued on or before the date of removal of goods.
- Section 12(3) Reverse Charge Mechanism (Goods): Time of supply is the earliest of:
  (a) Date of receipt of goods;
  (b) Date of payment recorded in books or debited in bank, whichever is earlier;
  (c) 31st day from the date of issue of invoice by the supplier.

  (a) If invoice is issued within the statutory period of 30 days under Section 31(2): Time of supply is date of invoice or date of payment, whichever is earlier.
  (b) If invoice is NOT issued within 30 days: Time of supply is date of completion of service or date of payment, whichever is earlier.

Analysis of Cases:

1. Date of receipt of goods: 14-09-2024.

Question 14. Determine whether the following independent persons are liable to take registration under the CGST Act, 2017:
1. Mr. Harish, a trader based in Jaipur (Rajasthan), deals exclusively in intra-State taxable supply of footwear. His aggregate turnover during the current Financial Year reached Rs. 38,50,000. He also has interest income of Rs. 2,20,000 from fixed deposits with commercial banks.
2. M/s Uttam Organics, located in Dehradun (Uttarakhand - Special Category State), is engaged in intra-State taxable supply of organic honey and herbs. Its turnover during the current FY is Rs. 18,50,000.
3. Mr. Devendra, a registered dealer in Madhya Pradesh, effects an inter-State taxable supply of handmade tribal handicraft products worth Rs. 45,000 to a customer in Gujarat. He holds a valid PAN and generates an e-way bill.

Answer 14.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Section 22 read with Notification No. 10/2019-Central Tax: The threshold limit for registration for an exclusive supplier of goods (intra-State) in normal states (such as Rajasthan) is enhanced to Rs. 40 Lakhs.
- Aggregate Turnover (Section 2(6)): Includes all taxable, exempt, export, and inter-State supplies under the same PAN. Under Central Tax clarifications, interest on loans/deposits (an exempt service) is NOT considered when testing whether a person is "engaged exclusively in the supply of goods" for the Rs. 40 Lakh threshold.
- Special Category States: For Uttarakhand, the threshold limit for supply of goods and services is Rs. 20 Lakhs (the enhanced Rs. 40 Lakh limit does not apply to Uttarakhand).

However, under Notification No. 03/2018-Integrated Tax, casual traders and artisans making inter-State taxable supplies of notified handicraft products are EXEMPT from compulsory registration if their aggregate turnover does not exceed Rs. 20 Lakhs (Rs. 10 Lakhs in Special Category States).
- Section 23(1)(b) Exclusive Reverse Charge Supplier: A person engaged exclusively in providing services where the entire tax is payable on reverse charge basis by the recipient under Section 9(3) is exempt from registration, regardless of turnover.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Evaluation of Liability for Registration:

1. Mr. Harish (Rajasthan):
- He is engaged exclusively in intra-State supply of goods (footwear).
- The receipt of bank fixed deposit interest (exempt service) does not disqualify him from the enhanced threshold of Rs. 40 Lakhs as per CBIC clarification.
- Turnover of footwear = Rs. 38,50,000 (which is <= Rs. 40,00,000).
- Conclusion: Mr. Harish is NOT liable for registration under Section 22.

2. M/s Uttam Organics (Uttarakhand):
- Uttarakhand is a Special Category State that opted for a Rs. 20 Lakh threshold for goods.
- Turnover during the year = Rs. 18,50,000.
- Since aggregate turnover is <= Rs. 20,00,000, the threshold is not crossed.
- Conclusion: M/s Uttam Organics is NOT liable for registration under Section 22.

3. Mr. Devendra (Handicraft Supplier):
- He makes an inter-State taxable supply of goods (Rs. 45,000).
- Ordinarily, Section 24(i) mandates registration without any threshold limit.
- However, for notified handicraft goods, Notification No. 03/2018-IT provides an exemption from compulsory registration up to the aggregate turnover threshold of Rs. 20 Lakhs, provided the supplier has PAN and generates e-way bills.
- Since total turnover is only Rs. 45,00,000 -> Rs. 45,000, it is well within Rs. 20 Lakhs.
- Conclusion: Mr. Devendra is NOT liable for registration.

4. QuickLogistics Ltd. (GTA under Reverse Charge):
- Entire turnover of Rs. 65,00,000 consists of GTA services where tax is payable exclusively under reverse charge by the recipient under Section 9(3).

1. Remember that bank interest does not destroy the benefit of the Rs. 40 Lakh goods threshold.

3. Providers of 100% RCM outward supplies are completely exempt from registration under Section 23.

2. Assuming the enhanced Rs. 40 Lakh limit applies uniformly across all states, ignoring exclusions for Uttarakhand, Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura.


Chapter 15: E-Way Bill & Tax Invoice / Credit Notes (Rule 138 & Section 31/34)

Question 15. Answer the following issues relating to E-Way Bills, Tax Invoices, and Credit Notes under the CGST Act, 2017 and Rules:
1. Prime Electronics Ltd., a registered supplier in Pune, sells a commercial LED display system to a buyer in Kolhapur:
   - Invoice Value of goods: Rs. 44,000.
   - GST charged on invoice @ 18%: Rs. 7,920.
   - Value of exempt accessories delivered in the same vehicle under a bill of supply: Rs. 12,000.
   State whether generation of an E-Way Bill is mandatory under Rule 138.
2. Calculate the validity period of an E-Way Bill generated on 15th January 2025 at 11:30 AM for transportation of non-over-dimensional cargo over a distance of 580 kilometres.
3. Dynamic Engineering Ltd. supplied industrial pumps on 20-08-2023 for Rs. 15,00,000 (tax invoice issued). In November 2024, the buyer complained of intrinsic manufacturing defects and returned goods worth Rs. 3,00,000. Can Dynamic Engineering Ltd. issue a Credit Note with GST adjustment for this return in November 2024? State the statutory time limit under Section 34(2).

[Marks: 6 | Time: 10 mins | E-Way Bill Rule 138 | Consignment Value | Credit Note Sec 34(2)]

Answer 15.
1. Applicable Provision / Section & Recent Amendment note:
- Rule 138(1) E-Way Bill Threshold: An e-way bill is mandatory before commencement of movement of goods in a vehicle if the "consignment value" exceeds Rs. 50,000.
- Consignment Value (Explanation to Rule 138(1)): The consignment value of goods shall be the value determined in accordance with Section 15, declared in the invoice/bill of supply/delivery challan, and includes taxes (CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess). However, it EXCLUDES the value of exempt supply of goods where invoice is issued in respect of both exempt and taxable supply of goods.
- Rule 138(10) E-Way Bill Validity: For non-over-dimensional cargo, the validity period is:
  - Up to 200 km: 1 day.
  - For every 200 km or part thereof thereafter: 1 additional day.
  - Each day is counted as the period ending at midnight of the day immediately following the date of generation.
- Section 34(2) Credit Notes: A registered person who has issued a credit note in relation to a supply of goods or services must declare the details of such credit note in the return for the month during which such credit note has been issued, but not later than:
  - 30th November following the end of the financial year in which such supply was made, OR
  - The date of furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Resolution of Issues:

1. Consignment Value Determination for Prime Electronics Ltd.:
- Taxable value of LED display: Rs. 44,000.
- GST @ 18%: Rs. 7,920.
- Subtotal Taxable Consignment Value = Rs. 44,000 + Rs. 7,920 = Rs. 51,920.
- The value of exempt accessories (Rs. 12,000) is excluded from the consignment value under the Explanation to Rule 138(1).
- Since the taxable consignment value including taxes is Rs. 51,920 (which exceeds the threshold of Rs. 50,000), generation of an E-Way Bill is MANDATORY.

2. Validity Period Calculation:
- Cargo type: Non-over-dimensional cargo.
- Total distance: 580 kilometres.
- Statutory formula: 1 day for first 200 km + 1 day for next 200 km + 1 day for part thereof exceeding 400 km (180 km):
  - Day 1: First 200 km (covers up to 200 km)
  - Day 2: Next 200 km (covers up to 400 km)
  - Day 3: Remaining 180 km (covers up to 580 km)
- Total Validity Period = 3 days.
- Counting: Generated on 15th January 2025 at 11:30 AM.
  - First day ends at midnight of 16th January.
  - Second day ends at midnight of 17th January.
  - Third day ends at midnight of 18th January 2025.
- Therefore, the e-way bill is valid until 12:00 midnight of 18th January 2025.

3. Credit Note Issuance by Dynamic Engineering Ltd.:
- The original supply was made in Financial Year 2023-24 (on 20-08-2023).
- Under Section 34(2), a credit note with GST tax adjustment can be issued no later than 30th November following the end of the financial year in which the supply was made (i.e., 30th November 2024) or the date of furnishing the annual return for FY 2023-24, whichever is earlier.
- The goods were returned in November 2024.
- Assuming the annual return for FY 2023-24 has not yet been furnished prior to the date of credit note issuance, Dynamic Engineering Ltd. CAN issue a credit note on or before 30th November 2024 and adjust its tax liability.
- If issued after 30th November 2024, a commercial credit note may be issued, but no reduction in output GST liability is permissible.

3. Final Conclusion:
- Issue 1: Mandatory to generate E-Way bill as consignment value of Rs. 51,920 > Rs. 50,000.
- Issue 2: Validity period is 3 days, expiring at midnight of 18th January 2025.
- Issue 3: Tax credit note can be issued up to 30th November 2024; after this cutoff date, GST liability cannot be reduced.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips:
1. Always add GST to taxable value when determining the Rs. 50,000 consignment value threshold.
2. Note the increased speed standard: 200 km per day (not the old 100 km per day).
