Chapter-1 Computation of Total Income & Tax Liability under Section 115BAC vs Old Regime

Question 1. Dr. Rajesh Sharma (aged 48 years), a resident individual practicing as a consulting physician in New Delhi, provides the following details of his financial transactions for the Previous Year 2024-25 (Assessment Year 2025-26):
1. Gross receipts from medical consulting profession: Rs. 42,50,000 (all received via account payee cheques or electronic clearing system).
2. Operating expenses claimed in Income & Expenditure account:
   - Clinic rent paid to landlord: Rs. 3,60,000.
   - Salary paid to clinic assistant: Rs. 2,40,000.
   - Purchase of diagnostic medical equipment on 10-11-2024 (put to use immediately): Rs. 4,00,000. (Normal depreciation rate applicable: 15%).
   - Cost of medicines purchased and administered: Rs. 6,20,000 (includes an invoice of Rs. 35,000 paid in physical cash on 14-08-2024).
3. Rental income from a commercial shop owned in Jaipur: Gross Rent received Rs. 35,000 per month. Municipal taxes paid by tenant: Rs. 15,000; Municipal taxes paid by Dr. Rajesh: Rs. 10,000. Interest paid on housing loan for construction of this shop: Rs. 85,000.
4. Long-term capital gains on sale of listed equity shares on 20-01-2025 (STT paid on acquisition and transfer): Rs. 2,25,000.
5. Savings bank account interest: Rs. 28,000.
6. Investments and payments made during the year:
   - Life insurance premium on own life (sum assured Rs. 5,00,000, policy issued in 2021): Rs. 45,000.
   - Contribution to Public Provident Fund (PPF): Rs. 1,00,000.
   - Health insurance premium paid via Net Banking for self and spouse: Rs. 28,000; for dependent father (aged 76 years, resident): Rs. 54,000.
   - Contribution to National Pension System (NPS Tier-I) under Section 80CCD(1B): Rs. 50,000.
Compute the Total Income and Net Tax Payable by Dr. Rajesh Sharma for AY 2025-26 under the Default Tax Regime under Section 115BAC(1A), and evaluate whether he should exercise the option to be taxed under the regular provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 14 | Time: 25 mins | Section 115BAC | PGBP & Total Income | High Yield | ICAI Fav]

Answer 1.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Under Section 115BAC(1A), as substituted by the Finance Act, the Default Tax Regime is applicable to all Individuals, HUFs, AOPs, and BOIs unless an option is exercised to shift out under Section 115BAC(6).
- Slabs under Default Regime for AY 2025-26: Upto Rs. 3,00,000: Nil; Rs. 3,00,001 to Rs. 6,00,000: 5%; Rs. 6,00,001 to Rs. 9,00,000: 10%; Rs. 9,00,001 to Rs. 12,00,000: 15%; Rs. 12,00,001 to Rs. 15,00,000: 20%; Above Rs. 15,00,000: 30%.
- Under the Default Regime: Chapter VI-A deductions (Sec 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), 80TTA) are not allowable. Deductions under Sec 80CCD(2) and Sec 80JJAA remain allowable.
- Disallowance under Section 40A(3): Payment in cash exceeding Rs. 10,000 a day to a person against an invoice is 100% disallowed.
- Depreciation under Section 32: Diagnostic medical equipment acquired and put to use for less than 180 days (acquired 10-11-2024) is entitled to 50% of normal depreciation (7.5%).
- LTCG under Section 112A: Taxable @ 10% on gains exceeding Rs. 1,00,000 without indexation.
- Section 87A Rebate: Under Default Regime, rebate up to Rs. 25,000 is available if total income does not exceed Rs. 7,00,000 (not applicable here as income > Rs. 7,00,000).

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Working Note 1: Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP)
- Gross Professional Receipts: Rs. 42,50,000
- Less: Allowable Professional Expenses:
  - Clinic Rent: (Rs. 3,60,000)
  - Salary to Clinic Assistant: (Rs. 2,40,000)
  - Cost of Medicines allowable [Rs. 6,20,000 - Rs. 35,000 disallowed u/s 40A(3)]: (Rs. 5,85,000)
  - Depreciation on Equipment [Rs. 4,00,000 * 15% * 50% (put to use < 180 days)]: (Rs. 30,000)
Net PGBP Income: Rs. 30,35,000

Working Note 2: Income from House Property (Commercial Shop - Jaipur)
- Gross Annual Value (Rent Rs. 35,000 * 12): Rs. 4,20,000
- Less: Municipal taxes paid by Owner: (Rs. 10,000)
  (Note: Municipal taxes paid by tenant are not deductible)
- Net Annual Value (NAV): Rs. 4,10,000
- Less: Deductions under Section 24:
  - Standard deduction u/s 24(a) @ 30% of NAV: (Rs. 1,23,000)
  - Interest on borrowed capital u/s 24(b): (Rs. 85,000)
Net Income from House Property: Rs. 2,02,000

Working Note 3: Capital Gains
- Long-Term Capital Gains u/s 112A on listed equity shares: Rs. 2,25,000

Working Note 4: Income from Other Sources
- Savings Bank Interest: Rs. 28,000

Gross Total Income (GTI):
= PGBP (Rs. 30,35,000) + House Property (Rs. 2,02,000) + LTCG (Rs. 2,25,000) + Other Sources (Rs. 28,000)
= Rs. 34,90,000

Comparative Tax Computation for AY 2025-26:
Particulars | Default Regime u/s 115BAC | Optional / Old Regime
--- | --- | ---
Gross Total Income | Rs. 34,90,000 | Rs. 34,90,000
Less: Deductions under Chapter VI-A: | |
- Sec 80C (PPF Rs. 1L + LIC Rs. 45k) | Nil | (Rs. 1,45,000)
- Sec 80D (Self/Spouse: Rs. 25k max; Senior Father: Rs. 50k max) | Nil | (Rs. 75,000)
- Sec 80CCD(1B) (NPS) | Nil | (Rs. 50,000)
- Sec 80TTA (Savings Interest capped) | Nil | (Rs. 10,000)
Total Income (Rounded u/s 288A) | Rs. 34,90,000 | Rs. 32,10,000

Tax Computation:
A. Default Regime (Sec 115BAC):
- Tax on LTCG u/s 112A: 10% of (Rs. 2,25,000 - Rs. 1,00,000) = Rs. 12,500
- Tax on Normal Income (Rs. 34,90,000 - Rs. 2,25,000 = Rs. 32,65,000):
  - Upto Rs. 3,00,000: Nil
  - Rs. 3,00,001 to Rs. 6,00,000 @ 5%: Rs. 15,000
  - Rs. 6,00,001 to Rs. 9,00,000 @ 10%: Rs. 30,000
  - Rs. 9,00,001 to Rs. 12,00,000 @ 15%: Rs. 45,000
  - Rs. 12,00,001 to Rs. 15,00,000 @ 20%: Rs. 60,000
  - Above Rs. 15,00,000: (Rs. 32,65,000 - Rs. 15,00,000) * 30% = Rs. 5,29,500
  Subtotal Normal Tax: Rs. 6,79,500
Total Base Tax: Rs. 6,79,500 + Rs. 12,500 = Rs. 6,92,000
Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4%: Rs. 27,680
Total Tax Payable under Default Regime: Rs. 7,19,680

B. Optional / Old Regime:
- Tax on LTCG u/s 112A: 10% of (Rs. 2,25,000 - Rs. 1,00,000) = Rs. 12,500
- Tax on Balance Income (Rs. 32,10,000 - Rs. 2,25,000 = Rs. 29,85,000):
  - Upto Rs. 2,50,000: Nil
  - Rs. 2,50,001 to Rs. 5,00,000 @ 5%: Rs. 12,500
  - Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10,00,000 @ 20%: Rs. 1,00,000
  - Above Rs. 10,00,000: (Rs. 29,85,000 - Rs. 10,00,000) * 30% = Rs. 5,95,500
  Subtotal Normal Tax: Rs. 7,08,000
Total Base Tax: Rs. 7,08,000 + Rs. 12,500 = Rs. 7,20,500
Add: Health & Education Cess @ 4%: Rs. 28,820
Total Tax Payable under Old Regime: Rs. 7,49,320

3. Final Conclusion:
The net tax payable under the Default Tax Regime is Rs. 7,19,680, whereas under the Old / Optional Regime it is Rs. 7,49,320. Dr. Rajesh Sharma saves Rs. 29,640 by remaining under the Default Tax Regime under Section 115BAC(1A). Therefore, he should NOT exercise the option to shift out to the regular provisions.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Dissect each head of income methodically. Always prepare working notes for PGBP and House Property first. Compute GTI, apply chapter VI-A deductions for both regimes, and then calculate tax slab-wise.
Time-Saving Tips: Memorize the cumulative tax up to Rs. 15,00,000 under Section 115BAC (which equals Rs. 1,50,000) so you only calculate 30% on the excess above 15 Lakhs!

Examiner Observation
Students frequently make errors by allowing Section 80C and 80D deductions under Section 115BAC. Remember that only 80CCD(2) and 80JJAA are allowed under the default regime. Also, ensure municipal taxes paid by tenants are excluded from house property deduction.

Chapter-2 Residential Status & Scope of Total Income (Section 6)

Question 2. Mr. Brett Lee, an Australian cricket commentator and citizen of Australia, visited India during Previous Year 2024-25 for IPL broadcasting and promotional tours. His presence in India during the relevant year and preceding years was as follows:
- Presence during PY 2024-25: Arrived on 15-04-2024 and departed on 10-07-2024 (87 days), and returned on 10-09-2024 and left on 15-10-2024 (36 days). Total days in PY 2024-25 = 123 days.
- Presence in preceding previous years: PY 2023-24: 110 days; PY 2022-23: 135 days; PY 2021-22: 95 days; PY 2020-21: 85 days; PY 2019-20: 40 days; Prior years: Nil.
During PY 2024-25, he received Rs. 22,00,000 in Australia directly from an Australian channel for commentary services rendered in India. He also earned dividend of Rs. 1,50,000 from shares in an Indian company credited to his Melbourne bank account.
Determine the residential status of Mr. Brett Lee for AY 2025-26 and compute his Gross Total Income chargeable to tax in India.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 14 mins | Residential Status | Section 6 | Scope of Income | High Yield]

Answer 2.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Under Section 6(1) of the Income-tax Act, an individual is a Resident in India if:
  (a) He is in India for a period of 182 days or more during the relevant previous year; OR
  (b) He is in India for 60 days or more during the relevant previous year AND 365 days or more during the 4 preceding previous years.
- Note: The relaxation of 60 days to 182 days applies only to Indian citizens or persons of Indian origin (PIO) visiting India. Mr. Brett Lee is a foreign citizen and not a PIO; therefore, the 60 days + 365 days test in Section 6(1)(c) applies to him.
- Under Section 6(6), an individual is Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) if:
  (i) He has been a non-resident in 9 out of 10 preceding previous years; OR
  (ii) He has been in India for 729 days or less during the 7 preceding previous years.
- Scope of Total Income under Section 5: A Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) is taxed on global income. An RNOR is taxed on income received/accruing in India, plus business income controlled from India. A Non-Resident (NR) is taxed only on income received or deemed to be received, or accrued or deemed to accrue in India.
- Section 9(1)(ii): Income from salaries/fees for services rendered in India is deemed to accrue or arise in India, regardless of where the payment is received.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Step 1: Test of Residency under Section 6(1):
- Stay in PY 2024-25 = 123 days (exceeds 60 days).
- Stay in 4 preceding previous years:
  - PY 2023-24: 110 days
  - PY 2022-23: 135 days
  - PY 2021-22: 95 days
  - PY 2020-21: 85 days
  Total stay in 4 preceding years = 425 days (exceeds 365 days).
Conclusion on Residency: Mr. Brett Lee satisfies the second condition of Section 6(1) and is therefore RESIDENT in India for PY 2024-25.

Step 2: Test of Ordinary Residency under Section 6(6):
- Total stay in 7 preceding previous years:
  (110 + 135 + 95 + 85 + 40 + 0 + 0) = 465 days.
- Since his stay in the 7 preceding years is only 465 days (which is <= 729 days), he satisfies the condition of Section 6(6).
Conclusion on Ordinary Residency: Mr. Brett Lee is Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) for AY 2025-26.

Step 3: Computation of Gross Total Income in India:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Taxability Reason
--- | --- | ---
Commentary fee received in Australia for services rendered in India | Rs. 22,00,000 | Deemed to accrue or arise in India u/s 9(1)(ii) since services were performed on Indian soil.
Dividend from Indian Company credited in Melbourne | Rs. 1,50,000 | Deemed to accrue or arise in India u/s 9(1)(iv) as dividend is paid by a domestic company.
Gross Total Income Chargeable in India | Rs. 23,50,000 | Both incomes taxable for RNOR.

3. Final Conclusion:
Mr. Brett Lee is a Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) for Assessment Year 2025-26. His Gross Total Income chargeable to tax in India is Rs. 23,50,000.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Check the citizenship first to avoid giving the 182-day relaxation to foreign citizens. Count days in 4 years and 7 years separately. Quote Section 9(1)(ii) for commentary income earned in India.
Time-Saving Tips: Write down the two basic tests and two additional tests as a mini-table. If 7-year stay is <= 729 days, directly declare RNOR without calculating the 9 out of 10 years test!

Examiner Observation
Students mistakenly assume foreign citizens visiting India get the 182-day relaxation under explanation 1 to Section 6(1). The 182-day relaxation is strictly for Indian citizens and PIOs.

Chapter-3 Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP - Disallowances & Depreciation)

Question 3. Apex Precision Tools Ltd., an Indian manufacturing company, reports a Net Profit of Rs. 84,00,000 in its Profit & Loss Account for the Previous Year ended 31-03-2025. On scrutiny of the accounts, the following items are noticed:
1. Purchase of raw materials includes Rs. 14,00,000 purchased from M/s Microtech Engineering, a registered Micro enterprise under MSMED Act, 2006 on 15-01-2025. There was a written agreement specifying a 60-day credit period. The payment was actually made on 25-04-2025.
2. Commission of Rs. 3,50,000 paid to a resident agent on 20-11-2024 without deduction of tax at source under Section 194H.
3. Interest of Rs. 2,00,000 paid to an NBFC on loan taken for business. Tax was deducted on 28-03-2025 but deposited to the Central Government on 15-11-2025 (after due date of filing return of income u/s 139(1)).
4. Expenditure of Rs. 60,000 on purchase of stationery paid on 12-07-2024 via bearer cheque.
5. New CNC Machinery costing Rs. 25,00,000 acquired and installed on 15-11-2024. Normal depreciation rate is 15%. Company opted for regular provisions.
Compute the taxable PGBP Income of Apex Precision Tools Ltd. for Assessment Year 2025-26.

[Marks: 10 | Time: 16 mins | Section 43B(h) | Section 40(a)(ia) | Section 32 | High Yield]

Answer 3.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 43B(h) (inserted by Finance Act 2023): Any sum payable by an assessee to a Micro or Small enterprise beyond the time limit specified in Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006 shall be allowed as deduction ONLY in the previous year in which such sum is actually paid.
- Section 15 of MSMED Act: Payment must be made within the agreed date, which cannot exceed 45 days from the date of delivery. An agreement specifying 60 days is legally restricted to 45 days.
- Section 40(a)(ia): 30% of any sum payable to a resident on which TDS was not deducted or, after deduction, has not been paid on or before the due date specified in Section 139(1), shall be disallowed.
- Section 40A(3): Expenditure incurred where payment is made otherwise than by account payee cheque/draft/ECS/prescribed electronic mode exceeding Rs. 10,000 in a day is disallowed 100%. A bearer cheque violates this rule.
- Section 32 & Additional Depreciation: CNC Machinery acquired and put to use for < 180 days gets 50% of normal depreciation (7.5%) and 50% of additional depreciation under Section 32(1)(iia) (10% out of 20%). The balance 50% additional depreciation is carried forward to the next year.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Computation of Business Income for AY 2025-26:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Amount (Rs.)
--- | --- | ---
Net Profit as per Profit & Loss Account | | Rs. 84,00,000
Add: Inadmissible Expenses / Disallowances: | |
1. Disallowance u/s 43B(h) - MSME payment: | |
- Agreed credit: 60 days; Maximum legal limit: 45 days. | |
- 45 days from 15-01-2025 expired on 01-03-2025. | |
- Payment made on 25-04-2025 (in next PY 2025-26). | Rs. 14,00,000 |
2. Disallowance u/s 40(a)(ia) on Commission: | |
- Rs. 3,50,000 * 30% for non-deduction of TDS u/s 194H | Rs. 1,05,000 |
3. Disallowance u/s 40(a)(ia) on NBFC Interest: | |
- TDS deposited on 15-11-2025 (after Sec 139(1) due date). | |
- Disallowance = Rs. 2,00,000 * 30% | Rs. 60,000 |
4. Disallowance u/s 40A(3) on Stationery: | |
- Paid by bearer cheque > Rs. 10,000 | Rs. 60,000 |
Subtotal of Additions | | Rs. 16,25,000
Total | | Rs. 1,00,25,000
Less: Allowable Deductions: | |
Depreciation on new CNC Machinery u/s 32: | |
- Normal Depreciation: Rs. 25,00,000 * 15% * 50% | (Rs. 1,87,500) |
- Additional Depreciation: Rs. 25,00,000 * 20% * 50% | (Rs. 2,50,000) |
Total Depreciation Allowable | | (Rs. 4,37,500)
Taxable Income under PGBP | | Rs. 95,87,500

3. Final Conclusion:
The taxable Business Income (PGBP) of Apex Precision Tools Ltd. for AY 2025-26 is Rs. 95,87,500. The disallowed MSME payment of Rs. 14,00,000 will be allowed as a deduction in the subsequent PY 2025-26 upon actual payment.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Apply Section 43B(h) strictly: written agreements cannot override the MSMED 45-day statutory ceiling. Disallow only 30% for resident TDS defaults u/s 40(a)(ia) (not 100%).
Time-Saving Tips: Remember that Medium enterprises are NOT covered by Section 43B(h)—only Micro and Small enterprises!

Examiner Observation
A common blunder is disallowing 100% under Section 40(a)(ia) instead of 30%. 100% disallowance applies only to non-resident payments under Section 40(a)(i).

Chapter-4 Capital Gains (Section 50C, 54, 54EC & 54F Exemption Regime)

Question 4. Mr. Rameshwar (aged 54 years, resident) furnishes the following details regarding the sale of capital assets during the Previous Year 2024-25:
1. On 10-08-2024, he sold an ancestral residential house in Pune for a declared consideration of Rs. 85,00,000.
   - Stamp duty value on the date of agreement (12-05-2024): Rs. 92,00,000 (an advance of Rs. 5,00,000 was received via RTGS on that date).
   - Stamp duty value on the date of registration (10-08-2024): Rs. 96,00,000.
   - Cost of acquisition on 01-04-2001 was Rs. 12,00,000. Brokerage paid on sale: 1%.
2. Out of the capital gains, Mr. Rameshwar invested in the following on 20-01-2025:
   - Acquired one new residential apartment in Mumbai for Rs. 40,00,000.
   - Acquired another residential flat in Pune for Rs. 25,00,000.
   - Subscribed to bonds of Rural Electrification Corporation (REC) Ltd. redeemable after 5 years on 28-02-2025 for Rs. 55,00,000.
Cost Inflation Index: FY 2001-02 = 100; FY 2024-25 = 363.
Compute the taxable Capital Gains of Mr. Rameshwar for Assessment Year 2025-26 under the regular provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 10 | Time: 16 mins | Section 50C | Section 54 | Section 54EC | High Yield]

Answer 4.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 50C: Where declared consideration is less than Stamp Duty Value (SDV), SDV is taken as Full Value of Consideration (FVOC). However, if SDV does not exceed 110% of declared consideration, the declared consideration itself is adopted.
- If agreement date and registration date are different, SDV on agreement date can be adopted provided advance consideration was received on or before the agreement date by specified banking modes (RTGS/cheque/ECS).
- Section 54 Exemption: Available to an individual/HUF on transfer of long-term residential house property. If long-term capital gain does not exceed Rs. 2 Crores, the assessee can invest in TWO residential houses in India (once-in-a-lifetime opportunity).
- Section 54EC Exemption: Investment in bonds of NHAI/REC/PFC redeemable after 5 years within 6 months from the date of transfer. Maximum deduction is capped at Rs. 50,00,000 in a financial year.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Step 1: Determination of Full Value of Consideration (FVOC) u/s 50C:
- Declared Consideration: Rs. 85,00,000. 110% of declared consideration = Rs. 93,50,000.
- Advance of Rs. 5,00,000 was received by RTGS on agreement date (12-05-2024). Hence, SDV on agreement date (Rs. 92,00,000) is considered.
- Since SDV on agreement date (Rs. 92,00,000) does NOT exceed 110% of declared consideration (Rs. 93,50,000), Section 50C does not trigger!
- Adopted FVOC = Rs. 85,00,000 (Declared Consideration).

Step 2: Computation of Long-Term Capital Gains before exemptions:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.)
--- | ---
Full Value of Consideration | Rs. 85,00,000
Less: Transfer expenses (Brokerage @ 1% on Rs. 85 Lakhs) | (Rs. 85,000)
Net Sale Consideration | Rs. 84,15,000
Less: Indexed Cost of Acquisition (Rs. 12,00,000 * 363 / 100) | (Rs. 43,56,000)
Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) before exemptions | Rs. 40,59,000

Step 3: Deductions & Exemptions under Sections 54 and 54EC:
- Since total LTCG is Rs. 40,59,000 (which is <= Rs. 2 Crores), Mr. Rameshwar is eligible to claim exemption for BOTH residential houses (Mumbai flat Rs. 40L + Pune flat Rs. 25L = Rs. 65 Lakhs total investment).
- Investment in Section 54 houses = Rs. 65,00,000, which exceeds LTCG of Rs. 40,59,000.
- Exemption u/s 54 is restricted to the amount of LTCG = Rs. 40,59,000.
- Investment in Section 54EC bonds (Rs. 55,00,000, capped at Rs. 50,00,000) is not needed since LTCG is already reduced to Nil under Section 54.

3. Final Conclusion:
Taxable Long-Term Capital Gain for Assessment Year 2025-26 is NIL.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Always test the 110% safe-harbour threshold under Section 50C before substituting SDV. Check the Rs. 2 Crore limit under Section 54 to allow two houses.
Time-Saving Tips: When Section 54 fully wipes out the capital gains, note that Section 54EC investment cannot create a capital loss; simply state that taxable capital gains become Nil.

Examiner Observation
Students often substitute SDV mechanically without checking the 110% tolerance band. Under Section 50C, if SDV <= 110% of sale consideration, the actual sale price is taken.

Chapter-5 Capital Gains on Listed Equity Shares (Section 112A vs 111A)

Question 5. Ms. Ananya sold the following securities through a recognized stock exchange during the Financial Year 2024-25:
1. 2,000 shares of Alpha Ltd. sold on 18-09-2024 @ Rs. 850 per share. STT paid on sale and acquisition.
   - Date of acquisition: 12-04-2016 @ Rs. 220 per share.
   - Fair Market Value (highest trading price) on 31-01-2018: Rs. 650 per share.
2. 1,500 shares of Beta Ltd. sold on 22-11-2024 @ Rs. 380 per share. STT paid.
   - Date of acquisition: 10-06-2024 @ Rs. 460 per share.
3. Units of an Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund sold on 10-01-2025 resulting in short-term capital gains of Rs. 1,40,000 (STT paid).
Compute the Net Capital Gains taxable in the hands of Ms. Ananya and specify the applicable tax rates under Sections 112A and 111A for AY 2025-26.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Section 112A | Section 111A | Grandfathering | High Yield]

Answer 5.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 112A: Tax on Long-Term Capital Gains on transfer of equity shares in a company or units of equity-oriented funds where STT is paid. Taxable @ 10% on gains exceeding Rs. 1,00,000 without indexation benefit.
- Section 55(2)(ac) (Grandfathering Mechanism): Cost of acquisition for assets acquired before 01-02-2018 is higher of:
  (a) Actual cost of acquisition; and
  (b) Lower of: (i) Fair Market Value on 31-01-2018; and (ii) Full value of consideration.
- Section 111A: Short-Term Capital Gains on transfer of equity shares or units of equity-oriented fund through recognized stock exchange where STT is paid is taxable @ 15% (subject to Finance Act amendments).
- Section 70: Short-term capital loss can be set off against both short-term capital gains and long-term capital gains.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Step 1: Computation of LTCG on Alpha Ltd. u/s 112A:
- Period of holding: 12-04-2016 to 18-09-2024 (> 12 months) -> Long-Term.
- Full Value of Consideration (2,000 * Rs. 850) = Rs. 17,00,000.
- Cost of Acquisition under Section 55(2)(ac):
  - Actual Cost (2,000 * Rs. 220) = Rs. 4,40,000.
  - Lower of FMV as on 31-01-2018 (2,000 * Rs. 650 = Rs. 13,00,000) and Sale Price (Rs. 17,00,000) = Rs. 13,00,000.
  - Higher of Actual Cost (Rs. 4,40,000) and Step (ii) (Rs. 13,00,000) = Rs. 13,00,000.
- LTCG u/s 112A = Rs. 17,00,000 - Rs. 13,00,000 = Rs. 4,00,000.

Step 2: Computation of STCG/STCL on Beta Ltd.:
- Period of holding: 10-06-2024 to 22-11-2024 (< 12 months) -> Short-Term.
- Sale Consideration (1,500 * Rs. 380) = Rs. 5,70,000.
- Cost of Acquisition (1,500 * Rs. 460) = Rs. 6,90,000.
- Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) = (Rs. 1,20,000).

Step 3: Net Capital Gains after Inter-Source Set-Off u/s 70:
- Short-Term Capital Gain on Equity Mutual Fund = Rs. 1,40,000.
- Less: STCL on Beta Ltd. set off u/s 70 = (Rs. 1,20,000).
- Net Taxable STCG u/s 111A = Rs. 20,00,00 @ 15%.
- Taxable LTCG u/s 112A = Rs. 4,00,000 (taxable @ 10% on excess over Rs. 1,00,000).

3. Final Conclusion:
Ms. Ananya has Taxable LTCG u/s 112A of Rs. 4,00,000 (Tax liability = 10% on Rs. 3,00,000 = Rs. 30,000 + Cess) and Net Taxable STCG u/s 111A of Rs. 20,000 (Tax liability = 15% on Rs. 20,000 = Rs. 3,000 + Cess).

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Apply the 3-step grandfathering formula under Section 55(2)(ac) without indexation. Set off STCL first against STCG taxable at 15% rather than LTCG taxable at 10% to minimize tax legally!

Examiner Observation
Students often apply indexation to Section 112A shares. Indexation is expressly prohibited for equity shares under Section 112A.

Chapter-6 Salaries & Income from House Property

Question 6. Mrs. Suniti Roy is an Executive Vice President at Zeta Pharmaceuticals Ltd., Kolkata (population > 40 lakhs). Her emoluments for PY 2024-25 are:
1. Basic Salary: Rs. 1,80,000 per month.
2. Dearness Allowance: Rs. 50,000 per month (40% forms part of retirement benefits).
3. Zeta Ltd. provided an unfurnished accommodation in Kolkata owned by the company throughout the year.
4. Zeta Ltd. paid health insurance premium of Rs. 32,000 on group mediclaim policy covering Mrs. Suniti.
5. She owns a residential house in Kolkata which was self-occupied during the year. Interest paid on housing loan borrowed in 2018 for acquisition was Rs. 2,15,000.
Compute Net Taxable Income under the heads Salaries and House Property under:
(a) The Default Regime under Section 115BAC; and

Answer 6.

Chapter-7 Clubbing of Income (Section 64 Provisions)

Question 7. Mr. Kameshwar, a resident individual having business income of Rs. 16,00,000, details the following transactions during PY 2024-25:
1. He gifted Rs. 5,00,000 to his wife Mrs. Priya on 01-05-2024. Mrs. Priya invested this entire amount in fixed deposit of a bank @ 8% p.a. interest. Interest earned of Rs. 36,667 was reinvested in another cumulative deposit which earned Rs. 1,500 interest during the year.
2. He transferred debentures of an Indian company worth Rs. 6,00,000 to his minor daughter Master Aniket (aged 14 years). Interest earned on debentures was Rs. 60,000.
3. His minor son Rohan (aged 16 years) won Rs. 1,00,000 in a national classical music competition.
Determine in whose hands the above incomes will be assessed for AY 2025-26 under the regular provisions of the Income-tax Act.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Clubbing | Section 64 | Minor Child | High Yield]

Answer 7.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 64(1)(iv): Income from assets transferred directly or indirectly to the spouse without adequate consideration is clubbed in the hands of the transferor spouse. However, interest on accretion of property (interest on interest) is NOT clubbed.
- Section 64(1A): All income accruing to a minor child is clubbed in the hands of that parent whose total income (excluding this income) is higher.
- Exception to Minor Clubbing: Income earned by a minor child on account of manual work or any activity involving application of his specialized skill, talent, or specialized knowledge is NOT clubbed.
- Exemption u/s 10(32): Exemption up to Rs. 1,500 per minor child is available under the Old Regime (disallowed under Section 115BAC).

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Taxability & Clubbing Provision
--- | --- | ---
Interest on Fixed Deposit from gifted funds | Rs. 36,667 | Clubbed in the hands of Mr. Kameshwar u/s 64(1)(iv) as funds arose from gift without consideration.
Interest on cumulative deposit (reinvested interest) | Rs. 1,500 | Taxable in the hands of Mrs. Priya. Accretion to transferred asset is NOT clubbed.
Interest on debentures of minor daughter Aniket | Rs. 60,000 | Clubbed in hands of Mr. Kameshwar u/s 64(1A) (his income is higher). Less: Rs. 1,500 exemption u/s 10(32) = Rs. 58,500 net clubbed.
Prize money won by minor son Rohan in music competition | Rs. 1,00,000 | NOT clubbed. Income earned through personal talent/skill is assessed directly in the minor's hands through legal guardian.

3. Final Conclusion:
Rs. 36,667 (wife's FD interest) and Rs. 58,500 (daughter's debenture interest net of Sec 10(32)) will be clubbed in the total income of Mr. Kameshwar.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Clearly distinguish between original asset income (clubbed) vs reinvestment income (not clubbed). Apply the talent/skill exception for the minor son.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently club minor child's talent/competition income. Any income earned through skill/talent is exempt from clubbing.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Set-Off | Section 70 | Section 71 | Section 35AD | High Yield]

Answer 8.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:

- Section 71 (Inter-Head Set-Off): Loss under the head House Property can be set off against any other head up to a maximum of Rs. 2,00,000. The unabsorbed loss exceeding Rs. 2,00,000 is carried forward.

Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Set-Off Treatment
--- | --- | ---

Approach: Check restrictions strictly: LTCL cannot be set off against STCG; Speculative loss is ring-fenced; Sec 35AD loss is ring-fenced; House property set-off is capped at Rs. 2,00,000.

Students frequently set off LTCL against STCG. Remember: STCL can be set off against both STCG and LTCG, but LTCL can ONLY be set off against LTCG.

Chapter-9 Chapter VI-A Deductions (Sections 80C, 80D, 80JJAA & 80CCD)

Question 9. Pragati Infotech Ltd., a resident Indian software company, provides the following data for Previous Year 2024-25:
1. Employs 120 regular software engineers as on 01-04-2024.
2. On 01-06-2024, it recruited 40 additional software developers on regular payroll at an emolument of Rs. 24,000 per month per employee paid by account payee bank transfer.
3. On 01-09-2024, it recruited another 20 employees at Rs. 28,000 per month per employee.
4. Gross Total Income of the company before Chapter VI-A is Rs. 1,25,00,000.
Evaluate the eligibility and quantum of deduction available to the company under Section 80JJAA for AY 2025-26. Is this deduction available under the concessional tax regime of Section 115BAA / 115BAC?

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Section 80JJAA | Additional Employee Cost | High Yield]

Answer 9.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 80JJAA: Deduction equal to 30% of additional employee cost incurred in the course of business for 3 assessment years starting from the year of employment.
- Conditions for "Additional Employee":
  (a) Total emoluments must not exceed Rs. 25,000 per month;
  (b) Must be employed for a minimum period of 240 days in the previous year (150 days in case of apparel/footwear/leather industry);
  (c) Must participate in recognized provident fund;
  (d) Emoluments must be paid by account payee cheque/draft/ECS/prescribed electronic mode.
- Concessional Regimes: Deduction under Section 80JJAA is uniquely ALLOWABLE even under Section 115BAA and Section 115BAC!

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Step 1: Evaluation of 40 developers recruited on 01-06-2024:
- Monthly salary: Rs. 24,000 (<= Rs. 25,000 threshold).
- Period of employment in PY 2024-25: 01-06-2024 to 31-03-2025 = 304 days (>= 240 days).
- Paid by account payee transfer.
-> Qualified as Additional Employees.
- Additional Employee Cost = 40 employees * Rs. 24,000 * 10 months = Rs. 96,00,000.

Step 2: Evaluation of 20 employees recruited on 01-09-2024:
- Monthly salary: Rs. 28,000 (exceeds Rs. 25,000 ceiling).
- Period: 212 days (< 240 days).
-> Disqualified under both criteria.

Step 3: Quantum of Deduction under Section 80JJAA:
- Qualifying Additional Employee Cost = Rs. 96,00,000.
- Deduction @ 30% of Rs. 96,00,000 = Rs. 28,80,000.
(This deduction of Rs. 28,80,000 will be available for 3 consecutive Assessment Years: AY 2025-26, AY 2026-27, and AY 2027-28).

3. Final Conclusion:
Pragati Infotech Ltd. is entitled to a deduction of Rs. 28,80,000 under Section 80JJAA for AY 2025-26. This deduction is allowable under both normal and concessional regimes (Sec 115BAA/115BAC).

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Check the two hurdles: (1) salary <= Rs. 25,000 p.m. and (2) days >= 240 days. Multiply eligible cost by 30%.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently forget that 80JJAA deduction is spread over 3 assessment years and is allowable even under Section 115BAA/115BAC.

Chapter-11 Scope of Supply (Section 7, Schedules I, II & III)

Question 11. Examine whether the following activities constitute a "Supply" under Section 7 of the CGST Act, 2017:
1. Reliance Industries transfers 50 laptop computers from its corporate office in Mumbai to its branch in Ahmedabad without consideration. Both offices have separate GSTINs.
2. An employee who resigns from XYZ Ltd. receives salary along with notice period pay recovery of Rs. 40,000 deducted by the employer.
3. Sale of a fully completed commercial complex where the entire consideration was received after the issuance of completion certificate by the municipal authority.
4. Disposal of obsolete factory equipment free of cost where input tax credit was availed at the time of purchase.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Scope of Supply | Schedule I | Schedule II | Schedule III | High Yield]

Answer 11.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 7(1)(c) read with Schedule I: Supply of goods or services between distinct persons (different GSTINs of same entity) when made in the course or furtherance of business constitutes a SUPPLY even if made WITHOUT consideration.
- Schedule I (Item 1): Permanent transfer or disposal of business assets where input tax credit has been availed on such assets is treated as a SUPPLY even without consideration.
- Schedule III (Item 5): Sale of land and, subject to clause (b) of paragraph 5 of Schedule II, sale of building where entire consideration is received AFTER issuance of completion certificate is NEITHER a supply of goods nor a supply of services.
- CBIC Circular on Notice Pay Recovery: Deductions for notice pay recovery from employee salary are not liable to GST as they are part of employment contract covered by Schedule III (services by employee to employer).

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Activity | Supply Verdict | Statutory Reason
--- | --- | ---
1. Laptops to Ahmedabad branch | YES (Supply of Goods) | Schedule I Para 2: Transfer between distinct persons without consideration in course of business is deemed supply.
2. Notice period pay recovery | NO (Non-Supply) | Covered under Schedule III Para 1 (employment contract). CBIC clarified notice pay recovery is not taxable.
3. Sale of completed building | NO (Non-Supply) | Schedule III Para 5: Sale of building after completion certificate is outside the scope of GST.
4. Obsolete equipment disposal | YES (Supply of Goods) | Schedule I Para 1: Permanent disposal of business asset on which ITC was availed is deemed supply.

3. Final Conclusion:
Activities (1) and (4) are taxable supplies under Schedule I; activities (2) and (3) are non-supplies under Schedule III.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Check Schedule I triggers: distinct persons & ITC on assets. Check Schedule III negative list triggers: employee services & post-completion building sales.

Examiner Observation
Students often confuse Schedule II (which only classifies whether an activity is goods or services) with Section 7 (which establishes whether an activity is a supply in the first place).

Chapter-12 Composite vs Mixed Supply (Section 8)

Question 12. Determine whether the following supplies are "Composite Supply" or "Mixed Supply" under Section 8 of the CGST Act, 2017, and specify the applicable rate of tax:
1. Hotel Grand Palace, Goa offers a 3 nights / 4 days vacation package for a consolidated price of Rs. 45,000 per couple, which includes luxury accommodation, buffet breakfast, airport transfers, and free usage of swimming pool. Individual tax rates: Accommodation: 18%, Breakfast: 5%, Cab transfer: 5%.
2. Mega Mart sells a Diwali Gift Hamper for a single price of Rs. 1,800 containing canned sweets (GST 5%), aerated fruit drinks (GST 28%), roasted dry fruits (GST 12%), and ceramic gift bowls (GST 18%).

[Marks: 6 | Time: 10 mins | Section 8 | Composite Supply | Mixed Supply | High Yield]

Answer 12.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 2(30) & Section 8(a): "Composite Supply" means a supply made by a taxable person to a recipient consisting of two or more taxable supplies of goods or services or both, which are naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business, one of which is a principal supply. It is treated as a supply of the PRINCIPAL SUPPLY and taxed at the principal supply's rate.
- Section 2(74) & Section 8(b): "Mixed Supply" means two or more individual supplies of goods or services made in conjunction with each other for a single price, which do not constitute a composite supply. It is treated as a supply of that particular supply which attracts the HIGHEST RATE of tax.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Case 1: Hotel Grand Palace Vacation Package
- Nature: The package consists of hotel accommodation bundled with breakfast and airport transfer. In the hospitality industry, breakfast and airport pick-up are naturally bundled with lodging.
- Principal Supply: Provision of hotel accommodation.
- Classification: COMPOSITE SUPPLY under Section 8(a).
- Tax Rate: Taxable @ 18% (the rate applicable to the principal supply, hotel accommodation).

Case 2: Mega Mart Diwali Gift Hamper
- Nature: The items (sweets, aerated drinks, dry fruits, ceramic bowls) can be easily bought and sold independently. They are not naturally bundled in the ordinary course of trade, and are supplied for a single composite price of Rs. 1,800.
- Classification: MIXED SUPPLY under Section 8(b).
- Applicable Tax Rate: Highest rate among the constituents:
  - Sweets: 5%
  - Dry fruits: 12%
  - Ceramic bowls: 18%
  - Aerated drinks: 28%
  Highest rate = 28%. The entire hamper price of Rs. 1,800 is taxed @ 28%.

3. Final Conclusion:
The hotel package is a Composite Supply taxed @ 18%. The gift hamper is a Mixed Supply taxed at the highest rate of 28%.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Test "natural bundling" first. If naturally bundled -> Principal rate. If independent goods sold for single price -> Highest rate.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently classify gift hampers as composite supplies. Since dry fruits and aerated drinks can be sold separately, they are mixed supplies.

Chapter-14 Input Tax Credit - Conditions & 180-Day Rule (Section 16)

Question 14. Sigma Heavy Engineering Ltd., a registered supplier in Maharashtra, furnishes the following inward supply details for September 2024:
1. Raw material received against tax invoice dated 02-09-2024 (GST paid Rs. 1,80,000). The material was received in three installments; the final lot was received on 05-10-2024.
2. Inward supply of components from Beta Ltd. against invoice dated 10-09-2024 (GST Rs. 75,000). Sigma Ltd. paid the invoice amount to Beta Ltd. on 15-04-2025 (217 days from invoice date).
3. Factory safety equipment received on 15-09-2024 (GST Rs. 45,000). Full payment made within 30 days. Invoice reflected in GSTR-2B.
Determine the eligible Input Tax Credit for the month of September 2024 and state the consequences of delayed payment to Beta Ltd. under Section 16(2).

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Section 16(2) | Installment Rule | 180-Day Rule | High Yield]

Answer 14.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 16(2) Mandatory Conditions for ITC:
  (a) Possession of tax invoice/debit note;
  (b) Details communicated in GSTR-2B u/s 38;
  (c) Actual receipt of goods or services;
  (d) Tax charged has been actually paid to Government;
  (e) Return under Section 39 (GSTR-3B) furnished.
- First Proviso to Section 16(2) (Goods received in lots): Where goods are received in lots or installments against an invoice, ITC can be availed ONLY upon receipt of the LAST LOT.
- Second Proviso to Section 16(2) (180-Day Payment Condition): If recipient fails to pay the supplier the invoice value plus tax within 180 days from the date of invoice, the amount equal to ITC availed must be REVERSED along with interest under Section 50. ITC can be re-availed upon actual payment.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Inward Supply | Amount (Rs.) | ITC Eligibility for Sept 2024
--- | --- | ---

3. Factory safety equipment | Rs. 45,000 | Fully eligible in Sept 2024.
Eligible ITC for September 2024 | Rs. 1,20,000 | (Rs. 75,000 + Rs. 45,000)

Consequence of Delayed Payment to Beta Ltd.:
- 180 days from 10-09-2024 expired on 09-03-2025.
- Since payment was made on 15-04-2025 (after 180 days), Sigma Ltd. must reverse ITC of Rs. 75,000 in GSTR-3B for March 2025 along with interest @ 18% p.a. from date of availing until reversal date.
- Upon payment on 15-04-2025, Sigma Ltd. can RE-AVAIL Rs. 75,000 in April 2025 (without time limit restriction).

3. Final Conclusion:
Eligible ITC for September 2024 is Rs. 1,20,000. Reversal of Rs. 75,000 with interest is required in March 2025, followed by re-claim in April 2025.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Track the last lot receipt date for installment purchases. Calculate 180 days precisely from invoice date.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently deny ITC in the month of purchase for items unpaid within 180 days. Initial credit is allowable; reversal happens only when 180 days elapse without payment.

Question 15. Vibrant Logistics Ltd., a registered supplier providing transportation and logistics services, provides the following details of taxes paid on inward supplies during October 2024:

2. 2 Heavy commercial trucks | Rs. 6,40,000 | Fully ELIGIBLE (goods transportation vehicle).
3. Outdoor catering for conference | Rs. 60,000 | Blocked u/s 17(5)(b)(i) (catering not for outward supply).
4. Mandatory Health Insurance | Rs. 95,000 | Fully ELIGIBLE (statutory requirement under Factories Act).
5. Lubricant oil destroyed by fire | Rs. 9,000 | Blocked u/s 17(5)(h) (goods destroyed by fire).
Total Eligible ITC for October 2024 | Rs. 7,35,000 | (Rs. 6,40,000 + Rs. 95,000)

3. Final Conclusion:
Vibrant Logistics Ltd. is entitled to an eligible Input Tax Credit of Rs. 7,35,000 for October 2024.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Check seating capacity: <= 13 seats passenger cars are blocked, but commercial trucks are ALWAYS eligible. Health insurance is eligible when statutory!

Examiner Observation
Students frequently block health insurance mechanically without noticing the exception where employer is mandated by statute to provide it.

Chapter-16 Time of Supply (Sections 12 & 13)

Question 16. Determine the "Time of Supply" under the CGST Act, 2017 in the following independent situations:
1. M/s Vardhaman Steels Ltd. enters into a contract to supply steel bars to a builder:
   - Date of removal of goods from factory: 10-10-2024.
   - Date of invoice issued: 15-10-2024.
   - Date of receipt of payment in bank account: 05-10-2024 (advance of Rs. 2,00,000).
   - Balance payment received: 25-10-2024.
2. Spark Media Ltd. provides digital advertising services to a client:
   - Date of completion of service: 12-08-2024.
   - Date of issuance of invoice: 20-09-2024.
   - Date of receipt of payment: 10-09-2024.

[Marks: 6 | Time: 10 mins | Time of Supply | Section 12 | Section 13 | Notification 66/2017 | High Yield]

Answer 16.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Time of Supply of Goods (Section 12 read with Notification No. 66/2017-CT): Taxable persons (except composition suppliers) are NOT required to pay GST on advances received for goods. Time of supply is earlier of:
  (a) Date of issue of invoice; OR
  (b) Last date on which invoice is required to be issued u/s 31(1) (date of removal of goods).
- Time of Supply of Services (Section 13):
  - If invoice is issued within 30 days from date of completion of service: Earlier of invoice date or payment receipt date.
  - If invoice is NOT issued within 30 days: Earlier of date of completion of service or payment receipt date.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Situation 1: Supply of Steel Bars (Goods)
- Date of removal = 10-10-2024 (Last date to issue invoice u/s 31(1)).
- Actual invoice date = 15-10-2024.
- Advance received on 05-10-2024 is exempt from advance GST under Notification 66/2017.
- Time of Supply for entire consignment = 10-10-2024 (last date invoice ought to have been issued).

Situation 2: Advertising Services
- Service completed on 12-08-2024. 30 days expired on 11-09-2024.
- Invoice issued on 20-09-2024 (after 30 days).
- Since invoice was delayed beyond 30 days, Time of Supply is earlier of completion date (12-08-2024) or payment date (10-09-2024).
- Time of Supply = 12-08-2024.

3. Final Conclusion:
Time of Supply for steel bars is 10-10-2024; Time of Supply for advertising services is 12-08-2024.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: For goods, ignore advance payment date due to Notification 66/2017. For services, check the 30-day invoice issuance test first.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently tax advances on goods. Remember: Notification No. 66/2017 exempts all regular taxpayers from GST on advances for goods.

Chapter-17 Value of Supply (Section 15 & Rules)

Question 17. M/s Shaurya Electricals Ltd., Pune supplies 100 Commercial AC units to Cool Comforts Ltd., Mumbai. The agreed list price per AC unit is Rs. 32,000 (exclusive of taxes). Additional terms:
1. Municipal transit tax levied by Mumbai Municipal Corporation: Rs. 40,000.
2. Pre-delivery inspection and testing charges paid by supplier: Rs. 25,000.
3. Special packaging required for transit: Rs. 35,000.
4. Cool Comforts Ltd. delayed payment, and Shaurya Electricals received late payment interest of Rs. 23,600 (inclusive of GST @ 18%).
5. Shaurya Electricals received a direct subsidy of Rs. 1,00,000 from the Maharashtra State Green Energy Development Authority, and another commercial subsidy of Rs. 50,000 from an NGO.
6. A trade discount of 5% on list price was agreed upon and recorded in the invoice.
Determine the Value of Supply under Section 15 of the CGST Act, 2017.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Value of Supply | Section 15 | Subsidies | Incidental Expenses | High Yield]

Answer 17.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 15(1): Transaction value is the price paid or payable where parties are unrelated and price is sole consideration.
- Section 15(2) Inclusions:
  (a) Any non-GST taxes, duties, cesses, fees charged separately;
  (b) Incidental expenses (packing, testing, inspection) before delivery;
  (c) Interest/late fee for delayed payment (treated as inclusive of GST);
  (d) Subsidies directly linked to price, EXCEPT subsidies provided by Central or State Government.
- Section 15(3) Exclusions: Discount given before or at the time of supply and recorded in invoice.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Particulars | Amount (Rs.) | Inclusion / Exclusion Reason
--- | --- | ---
List price of 100 AC units (100 * Rs. 32,000) | Rs. 32,00,000 | Base price.
Less: Trade discount @ 5% on list price | (Rs. 1,60,000) | Deductible u/s 15(3)(a) as recorded in invoice.
Net Base Price | Rs. 30,40,000 |
Add: Inclusions u/s 15(2): | |
- Municipal transit tax | Rs. 40,000 | Included u/s 15(2)(a) (non-GST tax).
- Pre-delivery inspection charges | Rs. 25,00,000 * 0 + Rs. 25,000 | Included u/s 15(2)(c) (incidental expense).
- Special transit packaging | Rs. 35,000 | Included u/s 15(2)(c).
- Late payment interest (excl. GST: Rs. 23,600 * 100 / 118) | Rs. 20,000 | Included u/s 15(2)(d). Interest is cum-tax.
- Maharashtra Govt Green Energy Subsidy | Nil | Excluded u/s 15(2)(e) (State Govt subsidy).
- Commercial subsidy from NGO | Rs. 50,000 | Included u/s 15(2)(e) (non-Govt subsidy).
Total Value of Taxable Supply | Rs. 32,10,000 |

3. Final Conclusion:
The Value of Taxable Supply under Section 15 of the CGST Act, 2017 is Rs. 32,10,000.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Add all non-GST taxes. Exclude Govt subsidies; include private/NGO subsidies. Back-calculate tax from late payment interest (Interest * 100 / 118).

Examiner Observation
Students often fail to back-calculate GST from late payment interest and include Government subsidies in the transaction value.

Chapter-18 Registration Thresholds & Compulsory Registration (Sections 22 & 24)

Question 18. Determine whether the following independent persons are liable to obtain registration under the CGST Act, 2017:
1. Mr. Alok of Uttar Pradesh is exclusively engaged in intra-state trading of electronic gadgets. His aggregate turnover during FY 2024-25 is Rs. 38,00,000.
2. Ms. Meera of Himachal Pradesh is engaged in trading of readymade garments. Her turnover during the year is Rs. 24,00,000.
3. Mr. Chetan of Gujarat, a non-registered farmer, sells raw cotton through a notified Agricultural Produce Marketing Committee (APMC) commission agent.
4. M/s Prithvi Enterprises of Madhya Pradesh makes an inter-state taxable supply of handcrafted wooden toys worth Rs. 1,50,000. Aggregate turnover is Rs. 12,00,000.

[Marks: 8 | Time: 12 mins | Registration | Section 22 | Section 24 | Notification 10/2019 | High Yield]

Answer 18.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Section 22 & Notification No. 10/2019-CT: Threshold limit for exclusive intra-state suppliers of GOODS is Rs. 40 Lakhs in normal states (UP, Gujarat, MP).
- Special Category States:
  - Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura: Rs. 10 Lakhs.
  - Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Puducherry, Telangana: Rs. 20 Lakhs.
  - Himachal Pradesh, Assam, J&K: Rs. 40 Lakhs for goods (as they opted for enhanced limit).
- Section 24(i): Persons making any INTER-STATE taxable supply of goods are compulsorily required to register, irrespective of turnover.
- Exemption to Inter-State Casual / Handicraft Goods (Notification No. 8/2018-IT): Persons making inter-state taxable supply of handicraft goods are exempt from compulsory registration under Section 24 provided aggregate turnover does not exceed Rs. 20 Lakhs (Rs. 10 Lakhs in special states) and PAN/e-way bill requirements are met.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Person | Turnover & Business | Threshold Applicable | Liable to Register?
--- | --- | --- | ---
1. Mr. Alok (UP) | Rs. 38 Lakhs (exclusive goods, intra-state) | Rs. 40 Lakhs | NO. Turnover (Rs. 38L) is below Rs. 40 Lakh threshold.
2. Ms. Meera (HP) | Rs. 24 Lakhs (exclusive goods) | Rs. 40 Lakhs | NO. HP adopted the enhanced Rs. 40 Lakh threshold for goods.
3. Mr. Chetan (Farmer) | Raw cotton cultivation & APMC | Section 23 Exemption | NO. An agriculturist is not liable to register for supply of produce out of cultivation of land.
4. M/s Prithvi (MP) | Rs. 12 Lakhs (inter-state handicraft toys) | Exemption Notif 8/2018 (Rs. 20 Lakhs) | NO. Benefitted by handicraft exemption from Sec 24; turnover <= Rs. 20 Lakhs.

3. Final Conclusion:
None of the four persons are liable to obtain registration under the CGST Act, 2017.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Check the special exemption for handicraft goods under Section 24 before declaring compulsory registration. Note that Himachal Pradesh has a Rs. 40 Lakh threshold for goods.

Examiner Observation
Students frequently mandate registration for inter-state handicraft goods under Section 24(i) without checking Notification No. 8/2018-Integrated Tax.

Chapter-19 E-Way Bill Provisions & Validity (Rule 138)

Question 19. Answer the following issues relating to E-Way Bills under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, 2017:
1. M/s Falcon Logistics transports a consignment of taxable machinery valued at Rs. 42,000 along with exempt agricultural produce valued at Rs. 20,000 from Nagpur to Raipur in a single motor vehicle under a single delivery challan. Is an E-Way Bill required?
2. Calculate the validity period of an E-Way Bill generated on Monday, 12th February at 11:00 AM for:
   - A normal cargo consignment moving a distance of 380 km.
   - An Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) consignment moving a distance of 85 km.

[Marks: 6 | Time: 10 mins | Rule 138 | E-Way Bill | Consignment Value | Validity | High Yield]

Answer 19.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Rule 138(1): E-Way bill is mandatory for movement of goods of consignment value exceeding Rs. 50,000.
- Explanation 2 to Rule 138(1): "Consignment value of goods" means the value declared in invoice/bill of supply/delivery challan including central tax, state tax, integrated tax, but EXCLUDING the value of EXEMPT SUPPLY of goods where the invoice is issued in respect of both exempt and taxable supply.
- Rule 138(10) (Validity of E-Way Bill):
  - Normal Cargo: 1 day for every 200 km or part thereof (amended from 100 km).
  - Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) / Multimodal transport including ship: 1 day for every 20 km or part thereof.
- Time of expiry: Midnight of the day up to which the e-way bill is valid.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Issue 1: Consignment Value Determination:
- Taxable machinery value: Rs. 42,000.
- Exempt agricultural produce value: Rs. 20,000 (excluded under Explanation 2).
- Net Consignment Value for E-Way Bill = Rs. 42,000.
- Since consignment value does not exceed Rs. 50,000, generation of E-Way Bill is NOT mandatory.

Issue 2: Validity Period Calculation:
A. Normal Cargo (Distance: 380 km):
- 1 day for first 200 km + 1 day for remaining 180 km (part thereof) = 2 Days.
- Generated on: Monday, 12th Feb at 11:00 AM.
- Day 1: Ends Tuesday, 13th Feb midnight.
- Day 2: Ends Wednesday, 14th Feb midnight.
- Validity expires at 12:00 Midnight of 14th February.

B. Over Dimensional Cargo (Distance: 85 km):
- 1 day for every 20 km or part thereof.
- 85 km / 20 = 4.25 -> 5 Days.
- Day 1: Tuesday midnight; Day 2: Wednesday midnight; Day 3: Thursday midnight; Day 4: Friday midnight; Day 5: Saturday midnight.
- Validity expires at 12:00 Midnight of Saturday, 17th February.

3. Final Conclusion:
Consignment 1 does not require an E-Way Bill (value <= Rs. 50,000). Consignment 2(A) validity expires at midnight on 14th Feb; Consignment 2(B) validity expires at midnight on 17th Feb.

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Remember the normal cargo rule: 200 km per day (not 100 km). Validity always ends at midnight, not at the generation hour!

Examiner Observation
Students frequently calculate validity as 24/48 hours from generation timestamp. Under Rule 138(10), validity runs up to midnight of the concluding day.

Chapter-20 Tax Invoices, Credit Notes & E-Invoicing (Section 31, 34 & Rule 48(4))

Question 20. Zenon Manufacturing Ltd., having an aggregate turnover of Rs. 18 Crores in FY 2023-24, raises the following queries regarding GST documentation and compliance:
1. Is Zenon Ltd. mandated to generate an E-Invoice under Rule 48(4) of the CGST Rules, 2017 for B2B supplies and exports?
2. On 10-06-2024, Zenon Ltd. supplied machine components to Beta Ltd. for Rs. 5,00,000 (GST Rs. 90,000). In September 2024, Beta Ltd. discovered defective components worth Rs. 1,00,000 and returned them. Zenon Ltd. wants to issue a Credit Note. What is the statutory time limit for issuing a GST Credit Note with tax adjustment for FY 2024-25?

[Marks: 6 | Time: 10 mins | Rule 48(4) | E-Invoicing | Section 34 | Credit Notes | High Yield]

Answer 20.
1. Applicable Provision & Recent Finance Act / GST Notification Amendment Note:
- Rule 48(4) (E-Invoicing Applicability): E-Invoicing is mandatory for all registered persons whose aggregate turnover in ANY preceding financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeds Rs. 5 Crores for B2B supplies and exports.
- Section 34(2) (Time limit for Credit Notes): A registered person who has issued a credit note in relation to a supply of goods or services must declare the details of such credit note in the return for the month during which such credit note has been issued, but not later than:
  - 30th day of November following the end of the financial year in which such supply was made; OR
  - The date of furnishing of the relevant Annual Return (GSTR-9),
  WHICHEVER IS EARLIER.

2. Step-wise Computation Table / Working Notes:
Issue 1: E-Invoicing Requirement
- Zenon Ltd.'s aggregate turnover in FY 2023-24 was Rs. 18 Crores, which exceeds the prescribed threshold of Rs. 5 Crores.
- Therefore, Zenon Ltd. is MANDATED to generate E-Invoices by reporting invoice details to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and obtaining an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and QR code for all B2B supplies and export invoices. Any invoice without an IRN is legally invalid.

Issue 2: Credit Note Issuance & Time Limit
- The supply was made in Financial Year 2024-25 (on 10-06-2024).
- The statutory outer time limit for issuing a GST Credit Note and reducing output tax liability is 30th November 2025 or the date of filing the Annual Return for FY 2024-25, whichever is earlier.
- Zenon Ltd. can issue a credit note for Rs. 1,00,000 + GST Rs. 18,000, and reduce its output tax liability in the monthly GSTR-3B return, provided Beta Ltd. reverses the corresponding Input Tax Credit.

3. Final Conclusion:
Zenon Ltd. must mandatorily issue E-Invoices with IRN. The Credit Note for returned goods must be declared on or before 30th November 2025 (or annual return filing date, whichever is earlier).

Approach & Time-Saving Tips
Approach: Remember the 5-crore threshold for E-Invoicing. The Credit Note time limit is 30th November (extended by Finance Act from the old 30th September limit).

Examiner Observation
Students frequently quote the old deadline (30th September) for credit notes. The amended statutory deadline is 30th November following the end of the FY.
