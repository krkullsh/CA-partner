### HOW THIS STRATEGY IS BUILT — AND WHY THERE ARE NO WEEKS

Most study planners assign a chapter to a week and move on regardless of whether the chapter has been truly mastered. That works for a theory-only subject, but Cost & Management Accounting is a numerical subject — mastery is a function of problems solved and concepts internalised, not calendar days passed. This strategy therefore has no week numbers. Instead, every chapter is given a Tier (priority), an exact number of written problems, MCQs, and oral-recall repetitions to complete before you consider that chapter 'exam-ready.' You move to the next chapter only when the current one's targets are met — whether that takes you two days or six.

- For theory answers: one-line definition → 3–4 explained points → one-line conclusion. This structure alone improves scoring on identical content.

### FINAL NOTES ON EXECUTION

Error Log — Your Single Most Important Document

Maintain one running Error Log across the entire syllabus. Every time you get a problem wrong, note: the chapter, the exact concept, whether it was a formula error, a presentation error, or a conceptual gap, and the correct approach. Review this log every week — it is the fastest route to converting weak chapters into strong ones, because it shows you your own specific error pattern rather than a generic one.

The 3-Day Rule (Apply Across All 15 Chapters)

Every 3rd day, spend 15–20 minutes reviewing the charts and formula sheets of chapters studied 3 days earlier — not re-reading the chapter, just glancing and actively recalling. This single habit, applied consistently across every chapter in this document, is what converts short-term understanding into exam-day recall.

When You Are Truly Exam-Ready

- Every Tier 1 chapter: written and MCQ targets met, and you can explain its core logic out loud without notes.
- Every Tier 2 chapter: written and MCQ targets met.
- Every Tier 3 and Tier 4 chapter: core format practised, common traps identified in your Error Log.
