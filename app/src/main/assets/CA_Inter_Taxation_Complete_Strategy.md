### How This Strategy Was Built — The Logic, Not Just the List

Most study plans hand you a chapter list and a study-hours number pulled out of thin air. This one doesn't. Every number in this document — how many written problems a chapter gets, how often you recite something out loud, which chapters are prioritised over others — is derived directly from what actually happened across the last 12 CA Inter Taxation exams (Dec 2021 to May 2026), cross-checked against the chapter-wise syllabus content. Nothing here is a guess.

Step 1 — Tiering by Frequency, Not by 'Feel' Every chapter was tagged with how many of the last 12 exams actually tested it. A chapter appearing in all 12 is fundamentally different from one appearing in 3 — the first is a guaranteed feature you cannot afford to under-prepare, the second is a contribution chapter you prepare efficiently but do not over-invest in. This produced four tiers: Tier 1 (appears in every or almost every exam), Tier 2 (8 or more of 12), Tier 3 (5 to 8 of 12), and Tier 4 (foundational chapters that rarely appear on their own but are embedded inside bigger questions).

Step 2 — Splitting Written Practice from Oral Revision by Question Type Not every chapter should be studied the same way. A chapter like PGBP or Capital Gains is tested through multi-step numerical problems — the only way to get fast and accurate at it is repeated written solving under time pressure. A chapter like TDS rate tables, GST return due dates, or the ITC blocked-credits list is tested through pure, fast recall — writing it out repeatedly wastes time that spaced oral repetition would use far better. So this plan assigns each chapter a written-problem count AND a separate oral-revision rhythm, calculated independently based on whether the chapter's marks come from calculation accuracy or memory accuracy.

Step 3 — Weighting by Recent Trend, Not Just the 12-Exam Average Two chapters — Value of Supply and Calculation of GST Payable — have small totals across all 12 exams but have scored a fixed, repeated mark value in each of the last two or three exams. A 12-exam average would bury this signal. This plan deliberately overrides the raw average for these chapters and treats a consistent, recent, locked-in pattern as more predictive than history — because an examiner who has tested something the same way three times running is telling you exactly what is coming next.

Step 4 — Matching Practice to the Exact Question Pattern, Not the Chapter Name Chapters are increasingly tested in combination — House Property merged with Clubbing, Dividend under both Companies-Act-style declaration logic and IT taxation, Supply and ITC and Payment fused into one 10-mark GST question. Wherever the exam has shown a specific combined pattern, this plan's written-practice instructions specify that exact combination rather than generic single-chapter problems, because solving chapters in isolation will not prepare you for how they are actually asked.

Step 5 — No Calendar, Open Timeline This plan deliberately does not assign weeks or dates. Work through Part A (Income Tax) and Part B (GST) chapter by chapter, in tier order if you want the highest-leverage chapters first (Tier 1, then 2, then 3, then 4), or in syllabus order if you prefer building foundation-up. Each chapter tells you exactly what 'done' looks like — the written-problem count and the oral-recall rhythm — so you can move to the next chapter only once a chapter's targets are actually met, at whatever pace your own calendar allows.

### Exam-Day Execution Strategy

Preparation only pays off if the 3-hour, 100-mark paper is executed with a plan. Taxation is really two subjects sharing one answer script — Income Tax and GST — and the biggest score losses happen from poor time allocation, not from not knowing the law.

Time Budget (3 Hours, 100 Marks)

Section Time Budget Rule if Running Over

Eliminate wrong options first; never Part-I MCQs (30M) ~36 minutes (72 sec/MCQ)

freeze on one MCQ.

If it overruns, cut from IT theory next Income Tax computation ~90 minutes

— never from GST.

Attempt in bullet/4-part format for Income Tax theory ~40 minutes

partial marks even if rushed.

GST yields more marks per minute — GST (all questions) ~50 minutes (incl. MCQ time)

protect this slot above IT theory.

The 10-Minute Move-On Rule If a problem hasn't yielded meaningful progress in 10 minutes, mark it, leave a working-note gap, and move on. Three partially-solved problems consistently outscore one perfect answer and two blanks. Practise this reflex in every mock so it becomes automatic under real exam pressure, not something you decide in the moment.

Non-Negotiable Answer Habits

- Always write the computation format neatly — marks are awarded for format, not only the final figure.
- In PGBP, always start from net profit per the P&L and show additions/deductions — never start from scratch.
- For every GST transaction, explicitly work through: taxability → rate → place of supply (CGST+SGST or IGST) → RCM applicability → ITC eligibility.
- In regime-comparison questions, explicitly state which deductions are allowed and which are disallowed under the default regime — this analysis itself carries marks.
- Working notes are mandatory and score independently — even a wrong final answer earns partial credit from correct working notes.
- Attempt every part of the compulsory questions (Q1 and Q5-equivalent) in full, even if only in rough or partial form — a blank part earns nothing, a rough attempt earns something.

### Master Pre-Exam Checklist

Before you consider any chapter 'done', it should pass this test — not a feeling of familiarity, but the ability to actually reproduce it cold.

- All Tier 1 chapters: written-problem target met AND the relevant table/checklist can be recited from memory without notes.
- PGBP and Computation of Total Income: at least one full 90-minute timed run-through completed for each, under both tax regimes.
- ITC: the 4-condition chain and full Sec 17(5) blocked-credits list can be written from memory in under 5 minutes.
- TDS/TCS table (rate, threshold, deductor) for all commonly-tested sections: reproducible from memory without hesitation.
- GST Payable (Ch. 30): at least 3 full end-to-end integrated problems solved without referring to notes.
- Every due-date table (Income Tax return filing + GST returns): reproducible from memory, not looked up.
- Capital Gains exemption chain (54/54B/54EC/54F) and slump sale (Sec 50B) mechanics: each solved at least twice independently.
- Cross-topic combinations practiced: House Property + Clubbing; Dividend (declaration + taxation); Supply + ITC + Payment integrated GST problem.
- At least one full 3-hour mock completed under strict exam time budget, with the 10-minute move-on rule actively used.
- All Tier 3 and Tier 4 chapters reviewed at least once — even 'low frequency' chapters are examinable; ICAI can test anything.

This is a logic-driven plan, not a countdown. Work chapter by chapter, tier by tier, at whatever pace your
