### Section 0 — How This Strategy Was Built (Read This First)

This is not a generic study plan. Every priority ranking, every marks figure, and every practice-count target in this document is pulled directly from three sources:

- The 12-attempt chapterwise marks table (Dec 2021 – May 2026), covering both old and new syllabus.
- The full question-by-question breakdown of the two most recent actual papers — Jan 2026 and May 2026 — so every technique below is anchored to a real, named question.
- The joint faculty strategy session (Advanced Accounting, Law, Taxation teachers) for exam-writing technique, cross-subject bridges, and revision method.

Why tiers, not weeks: a week-wise plan assumes everyone starts on the same day with the same number of hours available. A priority-tier plan does not. Whether you have 7 weeks or 7 months left, the correct order of attack does not change — only your pace through it does. Work Tier 1 to completion before starting Tier 2, and so on.

How the numbers were set: Written Practice = the number of full descriptive answers (in the 4-part format) you should write out by hand from past papers, RTP, MTP and mocks before you can call a chapter 'exam ready'. MCQ Practice = the number of MCQs to solve, scaled roughly 2x the written count because MCQs test the same law faster and across more fact patterns. Oral/Flashcard Rounds = the number of complete no-notes recall passes through the chapter's flash card or section-anchor list — these are frequency-driven: a chapter present in 100% of attempts needs continuous light-touch revision, not one big push.

### Section 1 — Understand the Paper Before You Study a Single Chapter

100 marks total: 30 marks MCQ (six independent case-scenarios of 4–6 marks each) + 70 marks descriptive (Q1 compulsory at 14 marks, Q2–Q6 optional — attempt any 4 of the remaining 5, each split into three parts of 4–5 marks).

The one fact that changes everything about Q1: In every single new-syllabus attempt (Sep 2024 to May 2026), Q1 has been a 3-part compulsory question testing THREE different laws at once — typically a mix of Companies Act + LLP or Companies Act + FEMA. You cannot pass this paper by mastering only one or two areas; Q1 alone forces cross-law fluency on Day 1 of the exam.

### Section 4 — Universal Answer-Writing Techniques (Apply to Every Chapter)

4.1 — The 4-Part Answer Format Use this structure for every single Law descriptive answer, regardless of chapter:

Part What Goes Here

1. Definition One-line meaning of the concept being tested

2. Section The exact section number governing the point

3. Provision The key rule/condition, stated clearly

4. Exception Any proviso or exception that applies

This format alone scores 80–85% of the marks on any Law question even when you are unsure of a minor detail, because it demonstrates legal reasoning rather than rote memory.

4.2 — The 'Yes, But' Technique for Scenario Questions Works for roughly 70% of practical/scenario-based Law questions — use it as the default approach whenever a question asks 'Can company/partner/person X do Y?'

Step Content

1. Yes / No Give the direct answer first

2. But State the condition or limit that applies

3. Because Cite the section or rule behind the condition

4. Therefore Restate the conclusion applied to the specific facts given

4.5 — The Flash Card System (For Ongoing Revision, Not First Study) One flash card per chapter — 15 cards total. On each card: chapter name, its top 5 sections with a one-phrase purpose for each, and one key exception. Once initial study of a chapter is complete, flip through all 15 cards in a single 15–20 minute pass, repeated regularly. This keeps every chapter alive without ever re-reading it in full.

4.6 — The Move-On Rule for Sequential (a)(b)(c) Questions Law questions are sequential — part (a), (b), (c) drawn from one scenario. A student stuck on part (a) for 15 minutes loses the easier marks sitting in parts (b) and (c). Always attempt all three parts, even if part (a) only gets a one-line 'Yes, But' answer. A partial answer to every part outperforms a complete answer to only one.

### Section 5 — The GCA + Interpretation of Statutes 'Free Marks' Track

These two chapters are treated separately here because the data justifies it: together they are worth a guaranteed, unchanging 16 marks every single attempt (8 + 8), they involve zero calculation, and they have never once been dropped from the paper in 12 attempts. Students consistently under-revise them because they feel 'small' — the data shows the opposite is true.

- Spend 2 fully focused days getting both chapters to 100% recall before moving to anything else in Tier 3 or below.
- GCA: master the 5 recurring MCQ themes (distance, notice, time, offence, notification power) plus Sec 9 and Sec 3(22).
- Interpretation of Statutes: master all 8 rules by name, one-line meaning, and one example each — nothing outside this set has ever been tested.
- Revisit both chapters in every single revision cycle from here to the exam — 10 minutes each, every few days. This is a maintenance chapter, not a study-once chapter.

### Section 8 — Exam-Readiness Checklist (Self-Assessment, Not a Calendar)

Use this to decide when a chapter is genuinely done — not when you feel like moving on.

Tier 1 (FEMA, LLP, GCA, Interpretation of Statutes) You can write a full 4-part / 'Yes, But' answer to ANY past-paper question from these four chapters, cold, in under 8 minutes, without opening notes.

Tier 2 (Audit, Management & Administration, Accounts, Share Capital & Debentures) You can reproduce every comparison table and flowchart for these chapters from memory, and correctly identify the section number for any core provision.

Tier 3 (Deposits, Dividend, Charges, Foreign Companies) You can solve the chapter's core numeric/procedural example correctly and identify which of the 2–3 recurring angles a new scenario belongs to.

Tier 4 (Prospectus & Allotment, Incorporation) You can answer the 1–2 specific angles that have historically been tested, even if you cannot answer every conceivable question from the chapter.

Tier 5 (Preliminary) You can define the 3–4 key terms. Nothing more is required.

Final rule before the exam: run one full 3-hour mock under strict exam conditions and check only two things afterward — (1) are your section numbers correct, and (2) is every answer in the 4-part format. These two factors predict your actual score more accurately than how much content you have read.
